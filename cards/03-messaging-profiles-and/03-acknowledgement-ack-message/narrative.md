Acknowledgement (ACK) messages are the authoritative application-level response to a submitted VXU transaction. While Section 2.5, Acknowledgement Processing Overview, establishes the foundational role of acknowledgements in maintaining trust and interface reliability, this section defines how ACK messages SHALL be interpreted within the VXU/ACK exchange pattern. The goal is to ensure that submitting systems and IIS share a consistent understanding of message disposition, required follow-up, and the meaning of acceptance and rejection (see Section 1.3, Key Terms, for the definition of Rejection).

At their most fundamental level, IIS provide a consolidated immunization record for each patient: an electronic version of what shots were received and when. A submitted VXU serves to update that record. Whether this core intent was fulfilled, that is, whether the submitted patient and their shot history (if submitted) could be associated with and recorded in the IIS, is the foundational concept that informs acceptance, rejection, and the structure of ACK messages throughout this section.

ACK messages serve two distinct but related purposes: they communicate whether the core intent of a submission was fulfilled, and they identify discrete issues that may require correction. Inconsistent interpretation of ACKs, particularly the relationship between MSA-1 and ERR segments, can result in unnecessary resubmissions, overlooked data quality problems, or misinterpretation of interface health.

This section defines the ACK interpretation model used throughout this guide. It establishes how core intent determines message disposition, how issue-level feedback is structured using ERR segments, and how acceptance, qualified acceptance, and rejection are determined and communicated.
