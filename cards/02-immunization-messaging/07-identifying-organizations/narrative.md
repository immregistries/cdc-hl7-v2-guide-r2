Accurate identification of organizations involved in immunization reporting is foundational to IIS operations. IIS rely on these identifiers to attribute data correctly, manage accountability, support program operations, and troubleshoot data quality issues. Incorrect or ambiguous organization identification can undermine trust in the data and create downstream operational risk.

Submitting systems and healthcare organizations are structured in many different ways. A single clinic may operate independently, or it may function as one unit within a larger parent organization such as a health system, pharmacy chain, or public health program. Technically, multiple organizations may share a single software instance or gateway, while legally they remain distinct entities. These organizational and technical structures do not always align with how IIS assign identifiers, creating the need for clear conventions and shared understanding.

This section defines how healthcare organizations are identified in immunization messages, how roles are distinguished, and how parent-child organizational relationships are represented. The goal is not to model every local hierarchy, but to ensure that each message clearly communicates who submitted the data, who is responsible for it, and when applicable, who administered the vaccination.
