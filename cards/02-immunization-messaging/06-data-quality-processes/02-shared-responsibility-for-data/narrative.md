Submitting systems and the organizations that operate them, together with IIS programs, share responsibility for the quality and completeness of immunization data. Many data-quality problems originate upstream from the IIS and cannot be corrected after the fact if the information was never collected or transmitted in the first place. A message can be technically valid according to the HL7 interface specification and still fall short of broader data-quality expectations, if important information is routinely missing or incomplete.

Submitting systems and the organizations that operate them are responsible for implementing workflows and system capabilities that support the consistent capture and transmission of high-quality immunization data [5]. This includes reviewing and acting on data-quality feedback received from the IIS, and working over time to reduce recurring issues to negligible levels.

IIS programs, in turn, are responsible for monitoring incoming data, identifying patterns of missing or problematic information, and communicating these issues to submitters. This communication typically happens both through acknowledgement feedback at the time a message is received and through broader, ongoing data-quality monitoring and reporting processes. IIS programs also play an important role in coordinating follow-up activities with submitting organizations to improve submission quality over time. The specific ways an IIS detects or classifies data-quality issues will vary depending on jurisdictional policy, system capability, and the operational context of individual data elements; the shared responsibility described here is about maintaining the reliability of the registry through ongoing monitoring, communication, and collaboration, not about prescribing a particular detection method.

Message conformance alone does not guarantee useful registry data. Sustained collaboration between submitters and IIS programs is necessary to maintain accurate and reliable immunization records over time. Where documented data-quality expectations are not met, whether by a submitting system or through gaps in IIS monitoring and follow-up, this may require corrective action through established onboarding, monitoring, or remediation processes.
