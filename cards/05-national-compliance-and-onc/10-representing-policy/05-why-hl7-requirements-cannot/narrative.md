HL7 requirements operate at exactly two moments: (1) when a product is certification-tested, and (2) when a message is submitted.

Data quality problems occur everywhere else. Bad addresses, filler names, missing lot numbers, outdated codes, and duplicate patients are not solvable by setting fields to R. The belief that "if we make it R, we'll get better data" consistently produces the opposite result.

Data quality is a continuous process-monitoring, onboarding, retraining, remediation, not a message-level constraint. HL7 conformance is a poor substitute for actual data quality work and should not be used as such.
