Recording adverse events following the administration of a vaccination can support multiple local use cases, including clinical review, analytics, and modifications to evaluation and recommendation logic. The national standard provides a mechanism for reporting these events to an IIS, but support for storing or using this information varies widely across jurisdictions. Some IIS may store these reactions, others may ignore them, and many cannot reliably maintain this information over time.

Adverse-event reporting in this guide is not intended to supersede or satisfy regulatory reporting obligations such as Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS). Reporting an event to an IIS does not initiate a VAERS report and does not meet VAERS reporting criteria; clinicians or organizations required to report to VAERS must do so via the VAERS process. The primary operational purpose of providing adverse-event observations to an IIS is to support clinical decision support and contraindication detection for future recommendations.

IIS should be treated as incomplete sources of adverse-event history. Critical safety information should be documented and maintained in the patient's medical record. When reactions are reported to an IIS and used in evaluation and recommendation workflows, the absence of such data must not be interpreted as evidence that no adverse event occurred.

Patients often receive multiple vaccinations on the same day, and the relationship between a reported reaction and any specific vaccination may be unclear. This guide does not provide a mechanism to indicate the strength of association or to adjudicate whether a reaction should be linked to one vaccination or many. If a reaction cannot be associated with a specific vaccination, it should be reported at the patient-level.
