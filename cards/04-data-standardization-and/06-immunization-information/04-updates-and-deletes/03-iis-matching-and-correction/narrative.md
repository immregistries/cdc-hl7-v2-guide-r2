RXA-21 expresses the submitting system’s intended operation but does not by itself establish whether a vaccination event is new or already represented in the IIS. An add can duplicate an existing event, and an update can fail to match the earlier report. IIS matching, deduplication, and consolidation therefore remain necessary for records sent as either A or U.

The combination of submitting source and ORC-3 provides strong evidence of source-record identity when the association is reliably maintained. It supplements rather than replaces patient matching and vaccination-level matching. An IIS can use RXA-21 to understand submitter intent without treating every A as automatically new or every U as automatically matched.

Additional guidance for vaccination-level matching, deduplication, and consolidation is provided in *MIROW Vaccination Level Deduplication in Immunization Information Systems* [10].
