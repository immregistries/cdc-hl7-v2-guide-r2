Immunization Information Systems (IIS) distinguish between administered and historical vaccination records to support data quality, inventory management, and record reconciliation. This distinction is frequently misunderstood. "Historical" does not imply that a dose was given long ago; it indicates that the reporting organization did not administer the dose and is reporting second-hand information. Conversely, "administered" indicates that the reporting organization gave the vaccination directly.

Consolidation does not require an IIS to disregard the relationship between a vaccination event and the records from which it was assembled. A consolidated vaccination event may be associated with records submitted by multiple systems, each with its own identifier. Retaining these source-specific associations can support later corrections and can provide querying systems with additional information for distinguishing records they previously reported from records received from other sources.

This distinction is defined for inbound reporting semantics (what a submitter reports to the IIS). See "Use of Administered vs. Historical on Output" for guidance on how IIS commonly present consolidated immunization histories in responses; querying systems should expect a history rather than rely on provenance flags. This distinction is important for submission workflows and IIS internal processing, but it does not represent a clinically meaningful difference once a complete immunization history has been consolidated.
