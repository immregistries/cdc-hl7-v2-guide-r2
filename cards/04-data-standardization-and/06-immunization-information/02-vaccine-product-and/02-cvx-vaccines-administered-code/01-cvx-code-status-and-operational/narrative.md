Each CVX code is assigned a status by CDC that reflects its relationship to vaccine products available for administration in the United States. These statuses describe product lifecycle and availability, not whether a code is valid for inclusion in a patient's immunization history. Misinterpreting CVX status as a validity indicator has historically led to inappropriate rejection of legitimate historical and foreign vaccination records.

CVX statuses are defined and interpreted operationally as follows:

**Active**: Vaccine formulations currently available for administration in the United States.

**Inactive**: Vaccine formulations no longer available in the United States, including category-level and Unspecified Formulation codes commonly used for historical records.

**Non-US**: Vaccine formulations available only outside the United States.

**Pending**: Vaccine formulations expected to become available but not yet active.

**Never Active**: Vaccine formulations that have never been available and should not appear in patient immunization histories.

Inactive and Non-US CVX codes are frequently required to accurately represent historical and foreign vaccinations. In limited cases, a vaccine administered outside the United States may not have a corresponding CVX combination code because the product is not licensed domestically. When this occurs, the vaccination may be represented by recording separate component vaccinations using appropriate Unspecified Formulation CVX codes. This approach is acceptable only for foreign vaccinations that lack a CVX representation and SHALL NOT be used for administered vaccinations or for historical vaccinations when CVX supports the known combination product.
