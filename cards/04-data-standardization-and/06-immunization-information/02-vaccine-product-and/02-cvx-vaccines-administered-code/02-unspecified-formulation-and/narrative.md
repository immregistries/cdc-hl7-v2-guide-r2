CVX includes codes that represent varying levels of specificity, including codes commonly used as Unspecified Formulation or category-level codes. These codes are essential for preserving immunization history when detailed product information is unavailable, particularly for historical and foreign vaccinations. Their use allows IIS to retain clinically meaningful records rather than rejecting incomplete data.

"Unspecified Formulation" in CVX is a conceptual designation, not strictly a categorical one. Some CVX codes function as both the most specific available representation and the appropriate Unspecified Formulation representation, because no more granular CVX exists. CVX 03 (MMR) is a common example: it is used both when reporting administered MMR vaccines and when reporting historical MMR vaccinations where no additional product detail is available. In contrast, other vaccine groups have multiple CVX codes at different levels of specificity, where use of a broader code may result in loss of clinically relevant detail.

Overuse of Unspecified Formulation or broader category-level CVX codes for newly administered vaccinations can undermine data quality by obscuring product specificity that is known at the time of administration. Because CVX intentionally supports both high and low granularity, IIS cannot rely on a fixed list of "invalid" submissions. Maintaining data quality therefore requires ongoing monitoring of incoming data to ensure that submitting systems are using the most appropriate level of specificity when possible.
