When reporting a newly administered vaccination in a VXU message, submitting systems are expected to convey sufficient information to accurately represent the vaccination event and allow the IIS to incorporate it into the patient's immunization history. This includes identifying the vaccine administered using supported code systems and providing product-level detail when available.

For administered vaccinations, CVX remains the canonical representation of the vaccination for immunization history, evaluation, and forecasting. NDC provides additional product-level detail that supports traceability, reconciliation, and audit workflows. IIS are expected to accept administered vaccination reports that include CVX, NDC, or both, provided the submitted codes are valid and supported. Submitting both CVX and NDC is considered good practice when available, but IIS must not require dual coding as a condition of acceptance.

Submitting systems are expected to report administered vaccinations at the highest level of specificity known at the time of documentation. For newly administered doses, this generally means reporting the exact product administered rather than a broad or category-level representation. Loss of specificity at the point of administration degrades data quality and limits the usefulness of immunization histories for downstream clinical and public health use.

When both CVX and NDC are submitted for an administered vaccination, the values are expected to be consistent representations of the same vaccination event. IIS are expected to inspect submitted values to ensure they match and to apply appropriate local handling when inconsistencies are detected.
