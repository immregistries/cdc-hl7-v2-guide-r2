Vaccine Information Statements (VIS) are federally required patient education documents that clinicians must provide before administering vaccines covered by the National Vaccine Injury Compensation Program (VICP), pursuant to the National Childhood Vaccine Injury Act (NCVIA), codified at 42 U.S.C. § 300aa-26. The statute requires healthcare providers administering covered vaccines to provide CDC-developed vaccine information materials to patients, parents, or legal representatives before each vaccine dose. VIS documents explain vaccine risks and benefits in accessible language and fulfill the statutory requirement to inform recipients of the benefits and risks of vaccination and the availability of VICP. These compliance obligations rest with the clinician and the organization administering the vaccination, not with the IIS.

Although VIS documentation is part of the administered vaccination record, IIS are not the system of record for VIS compliance. Clinical systems typically preload VIS information and associate it with CVX codes, ensuring consistent reporting regardless of how the VIS is delivered during the encounter. IIS may receive this information, but they do not determine whether the requirements of 42 U.S.C. § 300aa-26 were met.

VIS data provides limited value for IIS operations: VIS data does not affect evaluation, forecasting, vaccine validity, or the immunization history returned to clinicians. IIS should therefore align their data-quality expectations to their specific program needs and avoid using VIS fields as acceptance criteria for administered vaccinations.
