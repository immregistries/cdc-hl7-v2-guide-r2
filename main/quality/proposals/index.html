Proposed Analyses

The draft is already checked in several ways (listed below). This page proposes more comparisons and checks, in a suggested order, for CDC to consider. Each one would report its results the same way as the existing checks: findings with permanent IDs, a severity, and who acts (narrative, IGAMT, or a decision for CDC). Findings would appear on the sections they concern. Comments on any proposal are welcome. Use the comment panel and choose the proposal.

Every analysis here uses published documents, conformance profiles, and example or test messages only. None of them uses production data.

Checks already in place

  • Quality checks on the narrative: Structure and length of sections, conformance language, figures, references, and glossary pointers.
  • Narrative vs IGAMT: Does what the text says about fields, codes, and observations match the conformance profiles? Includes the answers to the Word review comments and the change requests for IGAMT.
  • Example messages: Each example is assembled into a whole message and checked against the IGAMT profiles.
  • TAB Comparison: Each published Technical Advisory Bulletin compared with the guide, requirement by requirement.
  • SISC Guidance: Underway. The SISC guidance documents published before the TABs, compared with the guide in the same way as the TABs (proposal 3 below).
  • Release 1.5 structural comparison: Underway. The Release 1.5 conformance profiles (from the NIST test tool) are loaded alongside the new IGAMT profiles so the two can be compared field by field.

Proposed first

These build directly on the comparisons already underway and answer the questions reviewers will ask first.

1 Alignment with the MIROW best practice guides pa-mirow

Why

The guide describes how an IIS processes what it receives: matching patients, deduplicating and consolidating vaccination records, active and inactive status, decrementing inventory by lot number, funding eligibility, and data quality. AIRA's Modeling of Immunization Registry Operations Workgroup (MIROW) has published consensus best practices on many of these same topics. Today the guide cites only one MIROW guide (Vaccination Level Deduplication in Immunization Information Systems), from one section. Where the two cover the same ground, they should use the same terms, should not contradict each other, and the guide should point readers to the MIROW guide rather than restate it.

Approach

  • List the current MIROW guides on the AIRA repository, and note which ones touch what is sent or received in messages.
  • For each of those guides, list its topics, defined terms, and recommendations that relate to messaging, and find the guide sections that cover the same topic.
  • Classify each pair: aligned, different wording (same meaning, harmonize the language), guide silent (add a link), or conflict (needs a decision).
  • Propose harmonized wording and glossary entries, and add each relevant MIROW guide to the Resources list with links from the sections it supports.
  • Present the results like the TAB Comparison, with one page per MIROW guide.

Compared with

  • MIROW best practice guides on the AIRA repository: for example vaccination-level deduplication, inventory management operations, data quality assurance, and patient active/inactive status
  • Validating Lot Numbers for Vaccine Doses (MIROW micro-guide, 2014, revised 2015). This may have been retired in favor of the 2022 Data Quality Assurance in Immunization Information Systems guide, which the guide already lists in Resources. Confirm which is current.

What CDC would get

A comparison page per MIROW guide, proposed wording changes for the narrative, new Resources entries, and "Related MIROW guidance" links on the affected sections.

Guide sections most likely affected

  • 2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication
  • 4.6.4.3 IIS Matching and Correction Processing
  • 3.2.5.6 Patient Demographic Records and VXUs Without Immunizations
  • 4.5.7 Patient Active Status in Clinical and IIS Contexts
  • 4.6.4.5 Inventory and Accountability Considerations
  • 4.6.5 Funding Source and Eligibility
  • 4.6.1.6 IIS Processing and Reconciliation
  • 5.10.5 Why HL7 Requirements Cannot Substitute for Data Quality

Scope: MIROW guides are best practices, not conformance requirements. The guide should link to them and use consistent language. It should not turn a MIROW recommendation into a SHALL unless CDC decides to.

2 Release 1.5 requirement trace pa-r15-trace

Why

The structural comparison finds differences in the profiles. Many Release 1.5 requirements, though, were written only in the narrative. Every one of them should be accounted for in the new guide, so that nothing is dropped without a reason.

Approach

  • Extract each conformance statement and normative sentence (SHALL, SHOULD, MAY and their negatives) from Release 1.5 and its published addendum.
  • Give each one an outcome in the new guide: kept, reworded, tightened, loosened, moved (for example into IGAMT), or dropped.
  • Report every requirement that was dropped or loosened without a stated reason.

Compared with

  • HL7 Version 2.5.1 Implementation Guide for Immunization Messaging, Release 1.5, and its addendum

What CDC would get

A trace table from Release 1.5 to the new guide, in the same format as the TAB comparisons, and findings for requirements dropped or changed without explanation.

3 SISC guidance documents published before the TABs pa-sisc-guidance

Why

Between 2016 and 2023, AIRA's Standards and Interoperability Steering Committee (SISC) published guidance documents that clarified Release 1.5 before the TAB series began. Implementers built to them, and two TABs build on them (the ACK TAB on the ACK guidance, and the CDS in RSP TAB on the message structure guidance). The guide covers their topics but does not cite any of them. Each one should be folded in, superseded on purpose, or cited, so nothing implementers rely on is dropped by accident.

Approach

  • Compare each document with the guide requirement by requirement, in the same format as the TAB comparisons.
  • For the ACK guidance and the national error codes draft, check only what the ACK TAB did not carry over, since that TAB is already compared.
  • Decide for each document whether the guide replaces it (say so in the change history) or should cite it (add it to Resources).

Compared with

  • Guidance for HL7 ACK Messages to Support Interoperability (v1.1, 2018)
  • Guidance for HL7 RSP Messages to Support Interoperability (MSA-1, QAK-2, ERR-4; v1.0, 2017)
  • Guidance on Detailed Message Structure and the Use of Specific LOINC Codes (v1.1, 2018)
  • Guidance on Populating OBX-14 (Observation Date-Time; v1.0, 2019)
  • Guidance on Indicating Preferable and Contraindicated Vaccines (v1.0, 2019)
  • Query Responses and Patient Protection (discussed with SISC, August 2016)
  • Vaccine Lot Number Guidance, for lot numbers that differ between unit of use and unit of sale (2017; updated 2018)
  • Guidance on National Set of Error Codes (draft for SISC review, April 2023)
  • Still to locate: Considerations for an IIS Implementing National Drug Codes, and Updated Guidance for Documenting Vaccine National Drug Codes (NDCs) and Lot Numbers

What CDC would get

A comparison page per document, findings for anything not carried into the guide, and new Resources entries or change history entries.

Guide sections most likely affected

  • 3.5.2.3 Z31 - Return a List of Candidates
  • 4.4.2.6 OBX-14 Date/Time of the Observation
  • 4.7.8.7 Preferred and Contraindicated Vaccine Types
  • 4.5.6.1 Protection Indicator (PD1-12) Usage
  • 4.6.2.3.1 Unit-of-Use and Unit-of-Sale NDCs
  • 4.6.2.4.3 NDC Requirements for Administered Vaccinations
  • 4.6.4.5 Inventory and Accountability Considerations

4 Who each change affects pa-change-impact

Why

Implementers will ask what they must change. A change list alone does not answer that. Each difference from Release 1.5 should say whether it affects senders, receivers, or both.

Approach

  • Classify each change in usage, cardinality, length, data type, and value set binding by its effect. For example, RE→R requires senders to change, R→RE or R→O can break receivers that rely on the field, and a removed code affects both.
  • Summarize the changes by profile and by actor (EHR or other sender, IIS).

Compared with

  • The Release 1.5 structural comparison (underway) and the requirement trace above

What CDC would get

A "What changes for implementers" summary with a table for senders and one for IIS, suitable for the ballot introduction and for SISC.

5 Backward compatibility, tested with messages pa-backward-compat

Why

Running messages through both sets of profiles shows the effect of the changes in practice. It shows which messages that are valid under Release 1.5 would now fail, and what a receiver still on Release 1.5 would reject.

Approach

  • Validate the Release 1.5 example messages and the published NIST test-tool messages against the new profiles.
  • Validate the new guide's example messages against the Release 1.5 profiles.
  • Trace each new failure to the change that causes it, and check that change is intended and documented.

Compared with

  • Release 1.5 examples and NIST test-tool test cases (published)
  • The new guide's example messages (Appendix A)
  • The existing example-checking tools, with the Release 1.5 profiles added

What CDC would get

A compatibility matrix (message × profile set), with every new failure linked to its cause.

6 Change history reconciliation pa-change-history

Why

The guide's change history should list every substantive change, and only changes that were actually made.

Approach

  • Match each difference found by the comparisons above to an entry in the change history.
  • Report differences that have no entry, and entries that match no difference.

Compared with

  • The guide's change history, and the results of the analyses above

What CDC would get

Findings on the change history, with suggested entries.

Next

Checks against the standards and code sets the guide depends on, and consistency within the guide.

7 Conformance to the HL7 v2.5.1 base standard pa-hl7-base

Why

A constrained profile may only narrow the base standard. If the guide loosens a usage, widens an HL7-defined table, or uses an incompatible length or data type, it would not conform, and the ballot would likely find it.

Approach

  • Compare each segment, field, and component in the IGAMT profiles with HL7 v2.5.1: usage (following the HL7 constrainable-profile rules for allowed changes), cardinality, length, data type, and table.
  • Check that each value set bound to an HL7-defined table is a subset of that table, or is clearly marked as an extension where extension is allowed.

Compared with

  • HL7 Version 2.5.1 (chapters 2, 3, 4 and 5, and the HL7 tables)
  • HL7 v2 conformance methodology (constrainable profiles)

What CDC would get

Findings on the profiles, mostly for IGAMT, and confirmation where the profiles conform.

8 Current code sets pa-code-sets

Why

Bound value sets drift as CDC and PHIN VADS publish updates. Retired codes that are still listed, or new codes that are missing, can cause valid messages to be rejected.

Approach

  • Compare each bound value set with its current published source: CVX, MVX, NDC, VIS document type, funding source, funding eligibility, and the others in PHIN VADS.
  • Report retired codes, missing codes, and description differences. Check any code named in the narrative or the examples too.

Compared with

  • CDC IIS code sets
  • PHIN VADS value sets referenced by the profiles

What CDC would get

Findings per value set, plus a note on how value sets will be kept current after publication.

9 National data standards published since Release 1.5 pa-data-standards

Why

Several national standards that affect patient demographics have changed since Release 1.5. The guide's value sets and guidance should either follow them or say why not.

Approach

  • Compare the guide with: the 2024 revision of the OMB race and ethnicity standard (SPD 15), the USCDI data elements for patient demographics (including sex and gender identity), and the CDC IIS Functional Standards.
  • For each data element, record whether the guide aligns, differs on purpose, or has not yet addressed it.

Compared with

  • OMB Statistical Policy Directive 15 (2024)
  • USCDI (current version)
  • CDC IIS Functional Standards

What CDC would get

An alignment table by data element, with findings where the guide is silent or differs.

10 Testability of each conformance statement pa-testability

Why

A conformance statement that no test can check will not be enforced consistently. Each statement should either map to a constraint in the IGAMT profiles or be marked as not machine-testable, with a reason.

Approach

  • Map each narrative conformance statement to an IGAMT constraint, predicate, usage, or value set binding.
  • Classify each statement as tested by the profiles, testable but not yet in the profiles (a change request for IGAMT), or not machine-testable (for example, IIS processing rules).

Compared with

  • The guide's conformance statements and the IGAMT export

What CDC would get

A coverage table, and change requests for IGAMT for the testable statements that are not yet tested.

11 Consistent terminology pa-terminology

Why

When a concept is renamed (for example, the guide describes "core intent" from the ACK TAB in terms of "Critical Data"), its meaning can change quietly. Key terms should mean the same thing everywhere: in the guide, the TABs, Release 1.5, and the MIROW guides.

Approach

  • For each key term and glossary entry, find every use across the guide and compare it with the definition.
  • Find terms in the TABs, Release 1.5, and the MIROW guides that the guide replaces with a different term, and record each mapping.

Compared with

  • The guide's key terms and glossary, the TABs, Release 1.5, and the MIROW guides

What CDC would get

Findings on inconsistent terms, and glossary updates (including "formerly called" notes where a term changed).

Exploratory

Wider comparisons whose scope should be agreed with CDC and AIRA before starting.

12 Survey of jurisdiction local implementation guides pa-local-igs

Why

Jurisdictions publish local guides that add to or override the national guide. When many of them add the same thing or override the same rule, the national guide is probably not specific enough there. That is useful input for this release.

Approach

  • Choose a sample of 5–10 publicly posted local guides that differ in size, region, and IIS platform.
  • Record what each one adds, tightens, loosens, or defines locally (for example, local fields, codes, or Z-segments).
  • Report the patterns across the sample, by topic, and the guide sections they point to.

Compared with

  • Publicly posted jurisdiction implementation guides

What CDC would get

A summary of common local additions and overrides by topic, with the guide sections that may need more detail.

Scope: The results would report patterns across the sample. They would not grade or rank individual jurisdictions.

13 Comparison with the AART assessments pa-aart

Why

AIRA's Assessment and Reporting Tool (AART) measures IIS against expectations derived from the national guide and the TABs. Where the new guide changes a requirement, an AART expectation may no longer match. Finding these early helps AIRA plan any updates.

Approach

  • Compare the published AART assessment expectations with the requirements of the new guide.
  • List the expectations that the new guide changes, removes, or does not support, and the new requirements that AART does not assess.

Compared with

  • AART assessment documentation (AIRA)

What CDC would get

A list of differences between AART and the new guide, for AIRA to consider.

Scope: This is a desk comparison only. Changing AART tests has an operational cost, so any updates to AART, and when they would happen, would be planned separately by AIRA. This analysis does not commit to them.
