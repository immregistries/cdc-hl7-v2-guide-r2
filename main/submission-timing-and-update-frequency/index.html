2.8.4 Submission Timing and Update Frequency

Quality review 1 note

Findings from automated and editorial checks, for the authors and reviewers. Not part of the guide.

  • Note 5 requirements were written as paragraphs in the draft and are shown here as conformance statements (wording unchanged): s1, s2, s3, s4, s5.qf-statement-from-paragraph-1c84d1a3

IIS maintain longitudinal immunization histories and depend on timely but stable submissions. Excessive updates or partial records increase processing burden and reduce data quality, while delayed submissions reduce the usefulness of the IIS record for clinical care and patient access. IIS expect vaccination events to be reported once the event record has been finalized within the submitting system, rather than receiving incremental updates as the record is being completed. This guidance is intended to prevent repeated submissions of the same vaccination event as data fields are progressively filled in, which increases processing burden and complicates deduplication. This guidance does not refer to not administered (NA) or partially administered (PA) dose indicators and does not prohibit legitimate clinical reporting practices.

Conformance statements

  1. SHOULDSubmitting systems SHOULD transmit administered vaccination records on the same day or within 24 hours of administration.s1
  2. SHALLSubmitting systems SHALL transmit only vaccination events after the event has been finalized in the source system.s2
  3. SHALL NOTSubmitting systems SHALL NOT send partial, in-progress, or repeatedly updated vaccination records for the same event.s3
  4. SHALLSubmitting systems SHALL support IIS-defined submission timing requirements when such requirements are published.s4
  5. SHOULDIIS SHOULD tolerate reasonable short processing delays while encouraging timely, stable submission of finalized vaccination events.s5
