2.6.1 Interpretation of RE ("Required by may be Empty")

This guide uses the standard HL7 v2 conformance designation RE ("Required, but may be Empty") and does not redefine its meaning (see section 2.9 Usage Designations and Operational Interpretations). RE should not be interpreted as optional or unimportant. Rather, it identifies data elements that systems are expected to support and provide whenever the information is available, even though that information may not always be present at the time of reporting.

For sending systems, RE means the system must be capable of transmitting the element and should populate it whenever the information is known within the clinical workflow. For receiving systems, RE means the IIS must be able to accept the element and process it meaningfully when it is present.

RE elements are discussed here because of their close relationship to data quality. Many data elements that matter for public health analysis and registry usability cannot be reliably enforced through message rejection, since the information may legitimately be unavailable in some circumstances, rejecting a message simply because an RE field is empty, would exclude otherwise usable data from the registry. Instead, IIS programs typically rely on broader data-quality processes to encourage consistent reporting of these elements: monitoring submission patterns, providing feedback to submitters, and working with partners to improve clinical workflows where gaps are identified.

This approach preserves the standard HL7 v2 meaning of RE while underscoring that these elements remain important, and it highlights the role of ongoing data-quality management, rather than message-level rejection, in supporting the effective use of RE fields over time.
