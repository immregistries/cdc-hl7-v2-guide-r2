4.5.7.2 Active and Inactive Status

Quality review 4 notes

Findings from automated and editorial checks, for the authors and reviewers. Not part of the guide.

  • Note MIROW guide Management of Patient Status in Immunization Information Systems (BR401, BR406 - Designations at the provider organization level): Reworded. The guide acknowledges the reasons but says they are "often operationally indistinguishable to IIS and should not be over-interpreted". That is consistent with MIROW's own note that many EHRs send no reason.
    Reworded
    Best practice Provider-level status is active, or inactive with one reason: no longer a patient, lost to follow-up, unspecified, or deceased. "Unspecified" is used only when an interface sends inactive without a reason.

    See the MIROW comparison

    qf-mirow-mps-03
  • Note Statement s2 MIROW guide Management of Patient Status in Immunization Information Systems (P308 - Explicit assignment takes precedence): Reworded. The guide treats provider-reported active and inactive values as "advisory rather than authoritative", which is weaker than MIROW's supremacy of an explicit assignment at the provider level. Both put deceased first.
    RewordedAction: CDC decision
    Best practice A status explicitly assigned by a provider organization of an acceptable type supersedes its previous status and any status the IIS has inferred, except that a confirmed death is not overridden.
    Recommendation CDC to decide whether an explicit PD1-16 value from the provider should replace the provider-level status the IIS holds for that provider (MIROW P308), and say so, or explain why the guide treats it as advisory.

    See the MIROW comparison

    qf-mirow-mps-05
  • Note Statement s1 MIROW guide Management of Patient Status in Immunization Information Systems (Acceptance of vaccinations): Guide adds. The guide makes explicit that status is never a reason to refuse a vaccination, including historical data for a deceased patient.
    Guide adds
    Best practice (MIROW assumes vaccinations are recorded whatever the patient's status; it does not discuss rejection.)

    See the MIROW comparison

    qf-mirow-mps-12
  • Note 2 requirements were written as paragraphs in the draft and are shown here as conformance statements (wording unchanged): s1, s2.qf-statement-from-paragraph-d4f91453

Active and inactive status values were originally designed to support a "medical home" model, in which a provider explicitly managed a defined patient panel and coordinated preventive services. While this model remains conceptually valid, modern EHR-centric workflows rarely include explicit status verification or reporting as part of routine immunization exchange.

Inactive status may be reported for a variety of reasons (e.g., patient moved, lost to follow-up), but these distinctions are often operationally indistinguishable to IIS and should not be over-interpreted.

Conformance statements

  1. SHALL NOTIIS SHALL NOT rely on active or inactive status as a prerequisite for accepting immunization events.⚑s1
  2. SHOULDIIS SHOULD treat active and inactive values as advisory rather than authoritative.⚑s2
