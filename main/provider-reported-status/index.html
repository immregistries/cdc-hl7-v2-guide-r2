4.5.7.1 Provider-Reported Status

Quality review 2 warnings · 4 notes

Findings from automated and editorial checks, for the authors. Not part of the guide.

  • Warning MIROW guide Management of Patient Status in Immunization Information Systems (Chapter 7, Table 8 - Coding provider-level status in HL7): Missing. The guide's patient status sections describe status only in general terms and never name PD1-16 or PD1-17, or say which code means what. The value set (HL70441) has the codes, with 'A' and 'I' required and 'L', 'M', 'P', and 'U' permitted, but a reader of the narrative cannot tell that 'M' is MIROW's "no longer a patient" or that 'P' signals a death.
    MissingAction: Narrative
    Best practice Provider-level status is sent in PD1-16 (immunization registry status), with its date in PD1-17: 'A' active, 'I' inactive (reason unspecified), 'L' inactive (lost to follow-up), 'M' inactive (no longer a patient), and 'P' permanently inactive, which MIROW reads as deceased once death is confirmed.
    Recommendation In Provider-Reported Status, name PD1-16 and PD1-17 and give MIROW's meaning for each HL70441 code (Table 8), including how 'P' relates to the deceased status.

  • Note Statement s1 MIROW guide Management of Patient Status in Immunization Information Systems (P312 - Any submission should include patient status): Partly covered. The guide treats status as optional in practice: PD1-16 is RE, absence of a status is not evidence either way, and no IIS may make status a condition of acceptance. It does not ask submitters to send it, which MIROW does.
    Partly coveredAction: Narrative
    Best practice Patient status should be included in any submission from a provider organization to the IIS.
    Recommendation Say that submitters SHOULD send PD1-16 (with PD1-17) when the provider maintains patient status, as MIROW P312 recommends, while keeping the rule that its absence means nothing.

  • Note MIROW guide Patient Status in Immunization Information Systems: Recommendations and Implementation Strategies (Section 2 (provider level), Appendix D - The HL7 fields for patient status, and why EHRs don't send them): Partly covered. The guide's profile has all these fields, but its patient status sections don't name them. That is the gap the report describes. Covered by the recommendations in mirow-mps-02 (PD1-16, PD1-17) and mirow-mps-06 (PID-29, PID-30), and by mirow-pbr-07 for PD1-11.
    Partly coveredAction: Narrative
    Best practice PD1-16 and PD1-17 directly identify the patient status and its date, and PID-30 and PID-29 report a death with its date. The RXA, PD1-11, and PD1-12 can be used to infer status. EHR vendors said MIROW's recommendations "are not part of the HL7 VXU transaction standard" or of EHR certification, so most EHRs don't know about them.
    Recommendation Apply mirow-mps-02 and mirow-mps-06, which bring MIROW's status codes into the guide that EHRs are certified against.

  • Note Statement s1 Its subject is "Absence of a patient status", not a system or party, so it is unclear who must comply. Naming the responsible party (for example, "Submitting systems SHALL …" or "IIS SHALL …") makes the requirement testable.qf-no-actor-236e7496
  • Note 1 requirement was written as a paragraph in the draft and is shown here as conformance statement (wording unchanged): s1.qf-statement-from-paragraph-7096a30b

Patient status values transmitted in HL7 v2 messages reflect the provider's perspective, not the IIS's jurisdictional view of the patient. An "active" designation indicates that the provider considers the patient currently under their care for immunizations. An "inactive" designation indicates the provider no longer considers the patient part of their active population. A deceased designation signals that immunization outreach should cease.

Because most interfaces submit data only at the time of vaccination, providers rarely send explicit updates when a patient becomes inactive. As a result, absence of an inactive status should not be interpreted as confirmation that the patient remains under active care.

Patient status values, when present, SHALL be interpreted as provider-reported information.

Conformance statements

  1. SHALL NOTAbsence of a patient status SHALL NOT be interpreted as evidence that the patient is active or inactive.⚑s1
