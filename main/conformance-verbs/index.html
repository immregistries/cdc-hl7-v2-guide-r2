1.5 Conformance Verbs

This guide uses a defined set of conformance verbs to indicate the strength of requirements placed on submitting systems, querying systems, and Immunization Information Systems (IIS). These verbs are capitalized when used normatively to distinguish them from narrative text.

The conformance verbs used in this guide are:

SHALL- A mandatory requirement. Systems claiming conformance to this guide are required to implement the stated behavior.

SHALL NOT - A mandatory prohibition. Systems claiming conformance to this guide are required to avoid the stated behavior.

SHOULD- A recommended requirement. Systems are strongly encouraged to implement the stated behavior but may deviate based on local policy, technical constraints, or phased implementation.

SHOULD NOT- A recommended prohibition. Systems are strongly discouraged from implementing the stated behavior but may do so if justified by local needs.

MAY- An optional capability or practice. Implementation is permitted but not required.

Claims of conformance to this guide require full adherence to all SHALL and SHALL NOT statements. SHOULD and SHOULD NOT statements are not binding but are intended to guide implementers toward consistent and interoperable practices over time. MAY statements indicate acceptable variations, optional behaviors, or anticipated local extensions.

Not all SHALL and SHALL NOT requirements in this guide are directly measurable or observable at the message-level. Some requirements apply to system behavior, processing logic, or operational practices of the submitting system, querying system, or IIS. These requirements are equally normative. Difficulty in testing or measurement does not reduce the obligation to meet them when claiming conformance to this guide.
