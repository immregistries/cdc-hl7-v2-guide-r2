What was asked

Questions in this section cover when OBX segments are required (administered vs historical doses, VXU vs RSP), how OBX-1 Set ID and OBX-4 Sub-ID should be numbered, how strictly OBX-2 value types should be enforced, and above all which LOINC observation to use in OBX-3. Within OBX-3 the recurring themes are VIS reporting (2-OBX GDTI method vs legacy 3-OBX method, and whether an IIS may require VIS), evidence of immunity vs contraindication codes, evaluation/forecast observations returned in Z42 responses (dose validity, vaccine type, series name, reason codes), and requests for new patient-level observations (COVID priority groups, occupation, disability, SOGI, school exemptions) that the national IG does not define. The AIRA/SISC "Guidance on Detailed Message Structure and the Use of Specific LOINC Codes" (Message Structure Guidance v1.1) is the most frequently cited reference.

Most questions in this part concern OBX-5 (Observation Value): which codes to send for vaccine funding program eligibility (64994-7) and vaccine funding source (30963-3), how to report VIS documents, how to code immunity/serology and contraindications, and how to structure dose evaluation and forecast observations in RSP (Z42) responses. Recurring themes are that OBX-5 is required whenever an OBX is sent (omit the segment rather than send it empty), that data should reflect what actually happened even when it conflicts with policy, local vs national code use, and standardizing CDS values (Y/N dose validity, LOINC answer lists for series status, unspecified CVX for vaccine groups). Smaller groups cover OBX-6 units for numeric observations, the meaning of OBX-14 (observation date/time), and OBX-17 (observation method, patient- vs dose-level eligibility).

OBX (segment-level)

Are OBX segments required for historical immunizations (RXA-9 not 00) in a VXU, and should an IIS require them?

Asked 2× · 2020–2022 · VXU · Answered · Related: RXA-9, RXA-20

Answer given

No. An IIS should not require observation segments for historical vaccinations.

The Guidance on Detailed Message Structure and the Use of Specific LOINC Codes (AIRA repository), section "Structure of the Send Unsolicited Immunization Update (VXU) - Z22 message" (page 3), lists the LOINC-coded observations that may be sent as part of a vaccination event. That document does not distinguish between administered and historical vaccinations; either can carry the same types of OBX information. The national IG adds constraints that require some of these OBX segments (e.g., funding eligibility, VIS information) for administered vaccinations, but there are no constraints or requirements for OBX segments on historical vaccinations. IIS should be prepared to receive any of these OBX segments and must be able to accept historical vaccinations with or without any of this information. Because the standard imposes no requirement, an IIS should only add local requirements for OBX on historical doses if it has a specific business use case that needs them.

OBX-1 Set ID

Why does an RSP with forecast OBX segments fail NIST conformance (AART Query Measure 3), sometimes with an error location that does not match the real problem?

Asked 2× · 2023–2024 · RSP · Answered · Fields: OBX-1 · Related: OBX-2, OBX-3, OBX-6, Conformance Testing & Validation Tools

Answer given

OBX-1 Set ID numbering was the cause in both cases.

  • In one case (2023) the OBX-1 values in the forecast OBX segments were not sequential (e.g., 16, 17, 19, 20). Renumbering sequentially cleared the error; the reported error location was misleading (the real problem was further up in the per-vaccine forecast OBX segments).
  • In another case (2024) OBX-1 must follow one of two patterns consistently: (a) a sequential number starting at 1 and incrementing by 1 for every OBX across the entire message without restarting, or (b) a sequential number starting at 1 within each order group (ORC/RXA/OBXs) and restarting at 1 for each subsequent order group. The IIS was using a hybrid (pattern a for the immunization history, pattern b for the forecast) and must choose one.
  • The 2024 case also had an OBX-6 problem: when OBX-2 is NM, OBX-6 (units) must be populated. For forecast OBX-3 30973-2 (dose number in series) a unit does not make sense, but HL7 still requires OBX-6, and the IG specifies NA from HL7 table 0353: `OBX-6 = NA^Not Applicable^HL70353`.

Can a VXU carry multiple VIS observation groups for one vaccination (e.g., English and Spanish versions, or one per component of a combination vaccine), and how should they be sent?

Asked 2× · 2021 · VXU · Answered · Fields: OBX-3 · Related: OBX-4, OBX-5

Answer given

Yes. A VXU can contain zero or more sets of VIS OBX segments for a vaccination, and as many as were actually given to the patient should be submitted.

Context: VIS documentation exists for the vaccine injury compensation program; CDC added it to HL7 v2 and ONC certification requires EHRs to send it, but most IIS are not the official medical record and many do not require it, so its national priority is expected to decline. The standard requires at least one VIS for most administered vaccines (some have none). VIS identifiers do not distinguish language; the patient should receive the latest version (English plus a possibly older translation), and the latest version should be documented. If more than one VIS is given (e.g., for combinations), all may be reported: each sheet is sent as a group of three OBX segments, so three sheets means nine OBX. Recommendations: send at least one VIS for every administered vaccination when known; send administered vaccinations even without VIS information (most IIS accept them); additional VIS may be sent.

Must an IIS reject administered doses that lack VIS OBX segments, and is there a requirement it can cite when a submitter objects to sending VIS information?

Asked 2× · 2021–2022 · VXU · Answered · Fields: OBX-3 · Related: OBX-5, Local Profiles & Jurisdictional Constraints, Data Quality & Validation

Answer given

The current IG does require VIS information for most administered vaccinations (except those with no VIS or equivalent). CDC added this requirement to support the requirement that VIS information be recorded in the patient's medical record for vaccine safety compliance, so the NIST tool requires VIS information and certifies that EHRs can send it; this can be cited. However, it has since been clarified that IIS are only required to collect and store VIS information when acting as the patient's medical record, and IIS may accept good administered data that lacks VIS. AIRA advises EHRs to report administered data even without VIS and let the IIS decide. Accepting data without VIS is a local policy decision, and many IIS do.

AIRA recommends citing the IG requirement only if VIS is also a business requirement for providers to participate with the IIS; the IIS need not enforce it simply because the guide lists it for proper message construction. IIS requirements should be driven by community needs for high-quality data, not by wanting perfect HL7 messages, and hard VIS requirements should not be set unless the needs and benefits can be clearly articulated. The IIS should accept either of the two IG methods for communicating VIS information and should not prohibit either.

Is a VIS publication date OBX required when VIS is sent with the document barcode (69764-9) plus date presented (29769-7, OBX-2 = DT)?

Asked 1× · 2024 · VXU · Answered · Fields: OBX-3 · Related: OBX-2, OBX-5

Answer given

No publication date is needed in this case. There are two ways to message VIS information: the 3-OBX method (vaccine type 30956-7, VIS publication date 29768-9, VIS presentation date 29769-7) and the 2-OBX method (VIS document barcode 69764-9 plus VIS presentation date 29769-7). The 2.5.1 IG prefers the 2-OBX method because it supports reporting the multi-vaccine VIS, which is impossible with the 3-OBX method; the 3-OBX method is legacy. IIS should support both, and AART uses the 2-OBX method. The VXU in question (which drew an IIS warning "VIS Identifier submitted value is not a valid option") passed NIST validation with no errors, including the date-presented OBX with DT.

Is it valid to send an OBX with 30956-7 (vaccine type) repeating the RXA CVX on a historical or non-combination immunization, or to indicate what vaccine was recommended?

Asked 1× · 2022 · VXU · Answered · Fields: OBX-3 · Related: OBX-5, RXA-9, RXA-5

Answer given

In a VXU, 30956-7 is used only to report the VIS vaccine type. For administered vaccines with a published VIS (or EUA fact sheet for COVID vaccines) the VIS information is required to be sent, though IIS generally are not required to record VIS unless they act as the official medical record, so most IIS do not require it but may store it. For historical immunizations VIS information is not expected but may be sent if known; there is no need to prevent it.

IIS do not expect information about what vaccine or combination was recommended, only what was given. An example such as `OBX|1|CE|30956-7^vaccine type^LN|1|160^Influenza H5N1 -2013^CVX||||||F` would confuse IIS, which would interpret it as VIS information and look for the accompanying publication and presentation date OBX; some IIS might reject the immunization. It is best not to include this OBX.

For a confirmed history of varicella (after lab review) where further varicella vaccination is "contraindicated", should OBX-3 be 30945-0 (contraindication) or 59784-9 (disease with presumed immunity)? When should each be used?

Asked 1× · 2021 · VXU · Answered · Fields: OBX-3 · Related: OBX-5, RXA-5

Answer given

It depends on what the provider is trying to tell the IIS, and the story supports several readings: (1) "laboratory results" implies a serologic test, which would be 75505-8 (serologic evidence of immunity); (2) "history of disease" suggests 59784-9 (disease with presumed immunity), often used for parent recall or unconfirmed (non-lab) documentation; (3) "further immunizations are contraindicated" suggests 30945-0 (vaccine contraindication). If the provider is messaging a fact about the patient (the patient had chickenpox), option 1 or 2 is best. If the provider is saying the vaccine was not given today because of a contraindication, 30945-0 is best. Having had chickenpox is not a contraindication; it is evidence of immunity (if it were a contraindication the patient would remain at risk), so 59784-9 or, given the lab results, 75505-8 is more appropriate. The test message (RXA-5 = 998, OBX-3 = 30945-0, OBX-5 = 33^VARICELLA INFECTION^NIP004) also labeled 30945-0 as "DISEASE WITH PRESUMED IMMUNITY", so code and description disagree. AIRA's message structure guidance maps what is being messaged to which LOINCs to use.

Must an IIS return patient immunity in Z32/Z42 responses, what is the correct format, and does order matter when multiple RXAs are returned?

Asked 2× · 2018–2021 · RSP · Answered · Fields: OBX-3 · Related: RXA, OBX-4, Immunization Evidence (refusals, immunity, contraindications), Query & Response Behavior

Answer given

There is no requirement that an IIS return patient immunity. If it does, in either Z32 or Z42, it should be returned under a "dummy" RXA (CVX 998, RXA-20 NA), e.g., followed by OBX 59784-9 (Disease with presumed immunity) with SNOMED 38907003 (Varicella immunity). Only one such dummy RXA is needed; all other patient-level information, such as the forecast, can follow it. There is no guidance on OBX order and it has no meaning; receivers should read them in any order. Recommendation: use an OBX-4 value unique within the message to distinguish this observation group, since receivers may use OBX-4 to group observations. An IIS no longer accepts contraindications/precautions via HL7 and fails AART Data Quality measures 17.3.1 and 17.4.1. Can it get an exemption?

Asked 1× · 2025 · VXU/ACK · Answered · Fields: OBX-3 · Related: OBX-5, Immunization Evidence, Conformance Testing & Validation Tools

Answer given

AIRA does not issue exemptions since it does not enforce compliance; the measures reflect meeting or not meeting community-defined standards. However, measures 17.3.1 and 17.4.1 only require the IIS to detect data quality problems in incoming VXUs (e.g., a contraindication date in the future or before the date of birth) and return an appropriate error in the ACK. Measure 17 does not require storing or returning contraindications/precautions. Similarly, 17.1 and 17.2 can be met by identifying issues on ingestion or correcting them later in the QBP response. So the IIS can pass without persisting the observations, if validation checks are implemented at intake.

Is there a standard way to return a patient adverse reaction (e.g., "Adverse reaction to measles vaccine") in an RSP so the querying system can display it?

Asked 1× · 2026 · RSP · Answered · Fields: OBX-3 · Related: OBX-5, RXA-5, Code Sets & Vocabulary

Answer given

Yes. Send an OBX with OBX-3 = 31044-1^Reaction^LN and a SNOMED CT code in OBX-5, under an ORC/RXA order group using CVX 998 (No vaccine administered) with RXA-20 = NA, as in the IG example:

``` ORC|RE||9999^DCS|||||||^Clerk^Myron| RXA|0|1|20090412|20090412|998^No vaccine administered^CVX|999||||||||||||||NA OBX|1|CE|31044-1^Reaction^LN|1|39579001^Anaphylaxis^SCT||||||F|||20090412 ```

The same pattern works for other coded adverse reactions with an appropriate SNOMED CT concept.

For a contraindication (30945-0), is the 30946-8 Contraindication effective date OBX required, and how does it relate to OBX-14 and RXA-3? Which date should the IIS use when they differ?

Asked 2× · 2024–2026 · VXU · Answered · Fields: OBX-14 · Related: OBX-3; OBX-5; RXA-3; Immunization Evidence

Answer given

Expectations for IIS processing are unclear in the national IG. There are three places a date can appear: RXA-3 (R), the date/time the report of the contraindication is being made; OBX-14 (RE), the physiologically relevant date/time such as onset of a condition; and an OBX with 30946-8 (OBX-3/OBX-5 both R), the date the contraindication/precaution is effective. AIRA's OBX-14 guidance (2024 answer) indicates submitters may send either OBX-14 or 30946-8 (OBX-14 need not be populated as long as a 30946-8 OBX accompanies it). Work with data exchange partners on what they send; jurisdictional law on authority to capture and share this information also matters.

A 2026 answer explains that OBX-14 is the date/time of the observation, i.e., when the fact was observed, not when the associated event happened (OBX was designed for lab results, where OBX-14 is when results were determined, not collection date or onset). So OBX-14 has limited value, which is why the effective date is sent separately (30946-8). A clinician may observe today a condition that has existed for some time, so the dates can differ. Use the effective date; in most cases ignore OBX-14 (applies to OBX-14 on 30945-0, 31044-1, 59784-9, 75505-8 observations).

With eligibility no longer in PV1, should an IIS keep capturing patient-level eligibility once dose-level reporting is in place?

Asked 1× · 2023 · VXU · Answered · Fields: OBX-17 · Related: OBX-5; PV1-20; VFC & Funding Eligibility

Answer given

Older guide versions sent patient eligibility in PV1-20, deprecated in favor of an OBX with each vaccination for finer granularity, since eligibility can differ by vaccine. The OBX method supports both: OBX-17 says whether eligibility was collected at the patient or dose level. An EHR that collects only patient-level eligibility should send it with each vaccination, marked as patient-level, instead of once in PV1. Best practice is to assess eligibility at each visit for each patient and vaccination. Patient-level reporting is kept for backward compatibility, and the guide's authors expected IIS to move to dose-level. AIRA asked what patient-level eligibility would be used for (e.g., reporting groups) in case current best practices don't cover a use case.
