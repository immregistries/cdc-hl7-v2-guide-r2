Immunization Evidence (Refusals, Immunity, Contraindications)

Working analysis for the authors, from questions asked of AIRA's technical assistance service. Not part of the guide.

24 questions. Findings: none
Not yet assessed

What was asked

This section collects questions about messaging information that supplements or substitutes for an administered dose. That includes vaccine refusals, deferrals and exemptions, contraindications/precautions ("patient conditions"), evidence of immunity (history of disease, serology), and adverse events. It also covers how booster/additional doses are identified. Recurring themes: - There is no standard way to message deferrals or exemptions. - IIS support for storing and returning refusals and contraindications is inconsistent, and some IIS reject refusals they do not support. - Confusion between refusals, deferrals, contraindications and immunity. - Message structure questions, such as how to send an OBX when no vaccination was given.

IG clarifications suggested when the TA records were consolidated (6)
  • Define how, or explicitly whether, to message deferrals and exemptions. The next IG draft defines conditions, refusals, deferrals and documented immunity, and states that deferrals are not reportable and must not be sent as refusals.
  • State that receivers not supporting refusals should acknowledge them with an informational (ERR-4 = I) error instead of rejecting them, and that administration-only elements (lot, VIS) are not required for refusals.
  • Describe contraindication observations as "patient conditions" not tied to a specific vaccine.
  • Address contraindication effective/expiration dates (no CDC data element exists today) and correcting or deleting erroneously sent observations (e.g., RXA-21 use).
  • Give clearer guidance and examples for refusals of combination vaccines, varicella history encoding (OBX-14 versus onset date), returning refusals/contraindications/immunity in RSP, and serology.
  • Provide stable value sets for contraindications, reactions, history of disease and serological evidence, and confirm their completeness.

Refusals

What counts as a vaccine refusal to document and report? For example: the patient refused the type of vaccine available, could not make an appointment, was unable to attend a mass vaccination event, or does not wish to attend mass vaccination.

Asked 1× · 2021 · VXU · Answered · Related: RXA-20, RXA-18

Answer given

None of those examples should be considered a refusal. Record a refusal only when the patient (or parent) actively refuses to receive a vaccination they were due for, typically in a face-to-face encounter with a provider. CDC originally had a separate "missed appointment" concept, but it ended up not being reported. In the message there is effectively no detailed reason for refusal. It is essentially a yes/no indication that there is evidence of a refused vaccine.

Some IIS return fatal errors, or block onboarding/production, when an EHR sends vaccine refusals/deferrals (RXA-20 = RE). Is the EHR doing anything wrong in sending refusals, and how should IIS handle refusals they do not support?

Asked 1× · 2021 · VXU/ACK · Answered · Related: RXA-20, RXA-18, ERR-4, Acknowledgement & Error Handling

Answer given

AIRA concluded that the EHR is not doing anything wrong in sending refusals, and IIS need to process them accordingly. As a matter of policy, an IIS may choose not to store some or all refusals, but it does not need to reject them. It can return an informational error (ERR-4 = "I") stating that refusals are not stored.

AIRA tested about 60 IIS: 49 accepted refusals, 6 did not, and 5 were not connected at test time. Rejection reasons included: - Missing vaccination event elements, for example lot number and VIS data being required even for a refusal. - Refusals not being supported. This was usually stated clearly in the ACK, but not always.

Some IIS that accepted refusals required the patient to already exist in the registry. At that time, two of the state IIS in question did not reject refusals in their test environments.

Deferrals and Exemptions

Is there a standard way to message vaccine deferrals or exemptions in HL7 v2? For example, an EHR sends a deferral as a contraindication (RXA-20 = NA with OBX 30945-0) and sends refusals with RXA-18 "Parental Decision". Is that correct, and is there a code for deferral?

Asked 4× · 2019–2026 · VXU · Answered · Related: RXA-18, RXA-20, OBX-3

Answer given

There is no standardized way to message exemptions or deferrals. - 2019: Deferrals are not normally messaged. They are not necessarily the same as contraindications or precautions, and there is no established way to message them. (One IIS that asked decided to drop the idea.) - 2020: The topic was discussed at an SISC meeting but never pushed forward. If there is interest, the community should define a standardized approach. - 2026: The current guide mentions deferrals but confuses them with contraindications. It provides no way to indicate that a patient was deferred from vaccination. It only supports reporting the contraindication that may support a deferral decision. Treating a contraindication as implying a deferral is not a terrible assumption, but it may or may not be true.

Draft language for the next IG distinguishes the following concepts: - Conditions: the collective term for contraindications and precautions. These are statements about the patient's state that may also be indications (e.g., pregnancy) and do not by themselves imply refusal, postponement or exemption. - Refusals: a recommended vaccine was offered and intentionally declined. This does not include postponement for illness, scheduling or practical reasons. - Deferrals: postponements where vaccination is expected later. These are useful locally but are not defined for reporting and should not be reported as refusals. - Documented immunity: prior disease or positive serology. - Operational non-receipt, such as vaccine out of stock: out of scope.

Implementers should check which elements their IIS accepts and make sure the reported concept matches the event.

What is AIRA's position on sending effective and expiration dates for contraindications ("not given") in HL7 messages to IIS, given that IIS appear not to consume them and the intent is undefined?

Asked 1× · 2021 · VXU · Answered · Related: OBX-3, OBX-5

Answer given

IIS do not receive "not given" vaccinations as such. They can receive temporary contraindications, but whether and how they record them varies. There are no CDC data elements for contraindication effective/expiration dates, so IIS generally do not receive or store them. An IIS may accept them, but EHRs are not certified for this, so implementation depends on the IIS. The HIMSS Immunization Integration Program test scripts include a test for a deferral due to fever with an expiration date.

Evidence of Immunity (History of Disease, Serology)

Is "degree of protection" against a disease the same concept as "immunization"? How does the US immunization messaging standard represent a patient's level of protection, given that protection may come from vaccination, from having had the disease, or from lab (titer) evidence?

Asked 1× · 2024 · VXU/general · Answered · Related: OBX-3, OBX-5, Code Sets & Vocabulary

Answer given

The US uses three concepts: 1. Completed vaccination series. A patient is assumed protected after completing a valid vaccination series (often through one of several valid paths). This is the normal route. 2. History of Disease. The patient, parent or clinician reports that the patient had the disease. In the US this is used mostly for varicella. A history of varicella disease removes the need for varicella vaccine but signals a future need for zoster vaccination. 3. Serological Evidence of Immunity. This records lab evidence. It is the most certain form, but vaccination systems rarely support recording it, and it is done only in specific cases, partly because lab tests can cost more than completing the series.

The national HL7 v2.5.1 immunization IG (the consolidated guide on the CDC IIS HL7 page) has value sets for History of Disease (printed page 361) and Serological Evidence of Immunity (printed page 362). These sets may not be complete, and not all IIS support them.

Adverse Events / Reactions

How should an adverse event (reaction) be reported when there is no associated vaccination event, or when the vaccination was not previously reported to the IIS? Must it be sent with an RXA segment?

Asked 2× · 2019–2021 · VXU/RSP · Answered · Related: OBX, OBX-3, RXA-5

Answer given

CDC IG Release 1.5 does not require an adverse event to be tied to a vaccination, or the vaccination to be reported to the IIS first. A message could report only that the adverse event happened, with no vaccination. Either way, it must be sent under an RXA, because in this version of the standard an OBX cannot be sent except under an RXA. If the adverse event is not tied to a vaccination event, send a placeholder RXA with RXA-5 = 998^No vaccine administered^CVX, as in the IG Appendix B example.

In an earlier (2019) discussion, after several options were researched, the approach settled on was similar to the IG's with minor improvements. The specifics were not recorded.

Booster and Additional Doses

If EHRs send doses without marking them as primary series or booster, how can an IIS tell whether an incoming dose (e.g., COVID) is a booster or an immunocompromised "additional dose"? Should submitters indicate this via OBX 30973-2 (dose number in series), and what algorithms should reports use?

Asked 2× · 2021 · VXU · Answered · Related: RXA-5, OBX-3

Answer given

From the provider-to-IIS perspective, the concept of a booster is not messaged at all. Providers just send doses, and the IIS consolidates the record and determines through its evaluation/forecasting whether a dose is a booster. OBX 30973-2 is not used in VXU submissions to signal booster status. ACIP's vote at the time was for an "additional dose", which is distinct from a "booster dose".

Dose numbers and "booster" labels are descriptions for humans, and CDS engines do not need them. A CDS engine looks only at dates and vaccine types and fits them to a set of possible schedules (catch-up, standard, 1- vs 2-dose, etc.). For COVID there are 1- and 2-dose schedules. An immunocompromised patient may receive an additional dose, so they have three doses: the first two complete the standard schedule, and the third does not invalidate it. Patients can receive more doses than needed, and the extra doses need not be evaluated.

Clinicians are not asked to submit dose numbers because: - The meaning of "dose number" is ambiguous: it could be a count of all doses, including invalid ones, or a position in a schedule. - The clinician may not know all prior vaccinations. - The distinction between "series" and "booster" is blurry. - Schedules change (e.g., ACIP could later define the COVID primary series as 3 doses).

The goal of CDS is to determine what the patient is due for next and when. Dose numbers and booster labels are communicated only to show how the engine placed the vaccine on the schedule.
