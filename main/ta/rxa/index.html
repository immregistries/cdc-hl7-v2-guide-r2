What was asked

Most questions in this part concern RXA-5 (Administered Code): how to send CVX and NDC together in a field that cannot repeat, which NDC (unit of use vs. unit of sale, 10 vs. 11 digits) to send and accept, how to handle CVX/NDC conflicts, how to code foreign, unspecified, inactive and COVID-19 products, and how CVX 998 is used to carry patient-level observations such as contraindications. Other recurring themes are partial or unknown administration dates (RXA-3), dose amount and units for non-volume or self-administered products such as oral typhoid (RXA-6/RXA-7), and the meaning and use of the information source codes in RXA-9 (administered vs. historical), including in query responses, IIS-to-IIS exchange and FHIR mapping.

Questions about the second half of the RXA segment cover who gave the dose (RXA-10) and where (RXA-11), lot numbers and expiration dates (RXA-15/16), manufacturers (RXA-17), refusal reasons (RXA-18), completion status (RXA-20), and the action code for add/update/delete (RXA-21). Recurring themes include how inventory decrementing depends on RXA-11.4 and lot numbers even though the VXU was not designed for it; accepting and flagging, rather than rejecting, doses given after the lot expired; how strictly to validate RXA-20 and RXA-17; and the lack of defined business rules for updates, deletes, and refusal records. Several questions come from newer scenarios, such as self-administered FluMist at Home, pharmacy technicians who have no NPI, and FHIR crosswalks.

When the exact administration date is unknown (only year, or year and month, are known), what date should be sent for a historical dose: should the record be withheld, or a date assigned?

Asked 4× · 2018–2022 · VXU/ACK · Answered · Fields: RXA-3 · Related: Data Quality & Validation

Answer given

Earlier responses (2018-2019) referred the asker to the MIROW (Modeling of Immunization Registry Operations Workgroup) best practice guidance on incomplete/unknown dates (confirmed with the MIROW steering committee as the best and most current reference) and to the local IIS; the rule text was not recorded.

A later response (2022) gave the substance: best practice is for immunization histories to use exact dates and specific vaccine codes, and the national IG does not support partial dates. Sites may send partial dates and the IIS can choose to accept or reject them. The MIROW community best practice suggests using the 15th of the month when year and month are known (applies only to vaccination event dates); the record should be flagged as estimated, although HL7 has no way to flag a date as estimated. The 15th minimizes the possible error to about 15-16 days. IIS should watch for an abundance of vaccination events on the 15th, since this practice should not be the norm.

For AART DQI test 18.2.2 (refusal submitted with an invalid date, e.g., before the birth date), what is expected? Returning a refusal RXA without a valid RXA-3 seems bad practice; and if the IIS rejects the refusal, should it have accepted it with a warning and returned the history without it?

Asked 2× · 2023–2024 · VXU/ACK/QBP/RSP · Answered · Fields: RXA-3 · Related: ERR-2, RXA-18, RXA-20, Immunization Evidence, Data Quality & Validation, Conformance Testing & Validation Tools

Answer given

There are two ways to pass most Data Quality Incoming/Ongoing checks. The easiest is to notice the problem in the incoming update and return an ERR segment pointing to where the problem is. For PID-8 gender, for example, return an ERR pointing to PID-8 when the code is missing or unrecognized, and the whole measure passes. The ERR severity does not have to be "E"; it can be "W" or "I", and the IIS can still process and map the data however it chooses.

AART checks the query response only when the ACK did not flag the problem. In that case it looks for evidence the data was handled properly, expecting the value to come back corrected. For the refusal date: returning a refusal RXA without RXA-3 would violate HL7. But if the IIS accepted the data without complaint, AART has to assume the refusal was stored without a date and should be returned that way.

The recommendation is to flag the issue in the ACK (a warning or informational ERR pointing to the field). Change query behavior only if desired. These problems are probably rare, so extra warnings should have little effect on live interfaces.

A later follow-up question (2024) about an IIS that rejected the refusal and returned "no patient found" in the RSP has no recorded answer.

An IIS rejects administered and historical vaccinations whose administration date is after the vaccine's end date (an IIS-maintained date). Would accepting them with a warning deviate from national standards?

Asked 1× · 2025 · VXU/ACK · Answered · Fields: RXA-3 · Related: RXA-5, ERR-4, Data Quality & Validation

Answer given

Clarify what "end date" means, as each has different implications: CVX codes have an active/inactive status, but inactive CVX codes are often valid for historical records and CDC expects them to remain usable for that purpose; NDCs have marketing end dates, but administration may legally occur after that date while inventory is still in use, and there is no federal guidance requiring rejection of post-end-date NDCs. There is no national standard prohibiting IIS from accepting vaccinations with inactive CVX or expired NDC codes; IIS make their own policy choices. Accepting these doses with a warning would not violate any known HL7 or CDC IIS guidance; it is a policy decision balancing data integrity and completeness. Rejection keeps invalid data out; acceptance with a warning allows analysis and provider follow-up. For an IIS-defined end date, at minimum the IIS should (1) indicate in the ACK that the vaccination is past the end date (as an error or warning) and (2) have data quality processes that detect and report sites sending vaccinations past the end date and follow up to correct the vaccine codes being used.

RXA-5 Administered Code

How can both NDC and CVX be sent in RXA-5 when it has a cardinality of 1..1? Is [1..2] or a repetition (~) acceptable, and why are two codes allowed if the field cannot repeat?

Asked 4× · 2016–2024 · VXU · Answered · Fields: RXA-5 · Related: Message Structure & Cardinality, Code Sets & Vocabulary

Answer given

No repetition is allowed. Only a single repetition of RXA-5 is allowed [1..1] per the base HL7 standard and the IG. To send both an NDC and a CVX for a dose, use the identifier and alternate identifier triplets built into the CE data type: CE.1-CE.3 carry the first code (identifier, text, coding system) and CE.4-CE.6 carry the alternate code (alternate identifier, alternate text, alternate coding system). Example: `49281-0560-05^Pentacel^NDC^120^DTaP-Hib-IPV^CVX`. A repeating form such as `20^DTaP^CVX^90700^DTaP, unspecified formulation^CPT~<NDC>^INFANRIX^NDC` is not allowed. The likely reason for constraining RXA-5 to one CE (primary plus alternate code) is to prevent a list of vaccines given at one visit from being reported in a single RXA. (The 2016 and 2024 askings of this question have no separate recorded answer.)

Should an IIS accept NDC codes as well as CVX in RXA-5? What should it consider, and do all IIS accept NDC?

Asked 6× · 2016–2025 · VXU · Answered · Fields: RXA-5 · Related: Code Sets & Vocabulary

Answer given

IIS must be able to accept CVX and/or NDC in RXA-5. Both NDC and CVX are acceptable in RXA-5 (confirmed, e.g., for COVID-19 vaccine NDCs). NDC use for administered vaccines is an ONC requirement for certified EHRs (2015 Edition criterion 170.315(f)(1)), while the CDC IG says CVX is strongly preferred; IIS should accept whichever code is sent, since both code sets support good vaccination data. AIRA shared its "Preparing for Meaningful Use Stage 3" overview resource.

Status of IIS support (2025, from AART Submission Assessment Measure 15, 11-digit NDC with hyphens): most IIS accept NDC if present; 41 IIS accept a message with only NDC; 11 require CVX to accompany the NDC (both in RXA-5); 3 cannot process the message if NDC is present and require CVX only. It is unknown whether IIS store the NDC; some map it to CVX or an internal identifier and lose it. AIRA does not disclose identified results, but IIS may share them with AART users under "My Peers". Practically, a sender must support sending CVX alone, CVX and NDC together, or NDC alone depending on the IIS. (NDC can be relevant, e.g., to distinguish self-administered FluMist.)

Are IIS expected to accept CVX, NDC, MVX and CPT codes from EHRs? Should the IIS crosswalk between them, and which code should it return in query responses?

Asked 1× · 2024 · VXU/RSP · Answered · Fields: RXA-5 · Related: RXA-17, Code Sets & Vocabulary

Answer given

In a VXU, the EHR may code the vaccine as CVX, NDC, or CPT, each with or without an MVX. MVX is a separate field (RXA-17) that captures only the manufacturer, not the vaccine type. The vaccine type comes from CVX, NDC, or CPT. These do not always map one-to-one, but CDC publishes mapping tables on its IIS code sets page.

When an EHR queries, the IIS usually returns the CVX for the vaccine even if another code was submitted, because CVX is the common denominator. The RXA-5 CE data type supports more than one representation, so a single vaccination can carry both NDC and CVX in RXA-5. EHRs often do this, and IIS can too.

Could an IIS normalize all incoming CVX/CPT/NDC codes to NDC (which carries more detail), store them, and return NDC in query responses? Is CVX as the common denominator a compliance requirement?

Asked 1× · 2024 · VXU/RSP · Answered · Fields: RXA-5 · Related: Code Sets & Vocabulary

Answer given

CVX is the common denominator because everything can be mapped to CVX, while not everything can be mapped to a specific NDC, even though some specificity is lost. For example, IPV is CVX 10, but a reported CVX 10 cannot be mapped to one NDC because it could be either of two NDCs. Some CVX codes have no NDC at all, notably the "unspecified" codes and the non-US codes. So normalizing everything to NDC does not work. Never throw away what was originally submitted: if NDC 50090-1693-09 (IPV) is received, do not store only CVX 10 and lose the NDC. When both are known, send both in RXA-5, e.g., `|10^IPV^CVX^50090-1693-09^IPOL^NDC|`.

Why are EHRs required to use the CDC NDC Linker for ONC certification when FHIR US Core only requires CVX? If an EHR always sends CVX, can it map CVX to NDC?

Asked 1× · 2025 · VXU · Answered · Fields: RXA-5

Answer given

US Core is FHIR-based and focused on making immunization data available for access, requiring CVX with NDC as a SHOULD translation. ONC certification requirements for IIS reporting are based on HL7 v2, under which newly administered immunizations must be reported using NDC codes; the HL7 v2.5.1 IG also says support for CVX is strongly preferred, and some IIS constrain this to require CVX along with NDC for administered doses. The CDC NDC Linker table translates an NDC (e.g., scanned from packaging) to its CVX. Always sending CVX to IIS is a good strategy. The linker can technically be used in reverse, but it is generally used from the more specific NDC to the less specific CVX; one CVX (e.g., 03 MMR) maps to several NDCs, so CVX-to-NDC is not a unique mapping.

For a multicomponent vaccine kit such as Penbraya (MenACWY vial NDC 00069-0271-01 and MenB syringe NDC 00069-0332-01), which NDC should the EHR report?

Asked 1× · 2024 · VXU · Answered · Fields: RXA-5 · Related: Code Sets & Vocabulary

EHRs are required to use NDC when reporting administered immunizations under the ONC certified health IT program. A vaccine can have a unit-of-sale (UoS) and a unit-of-use (UoU) NDC; UoU is preferred, but IIS should be able to receive either. How should an IIS process NDC and CVX when one or both are present or when they disagree (e.g., same vaccine group vs. different vaccine groups)? Should it process silently, return an I/W/E, or reject?

Asked 2× · 2019–2024 · VXU/ACK · Answered · Fields: RXA-5 · Related: ERR-4, Data Quality & Validation, Acknowledgement & Error Handling

Answer given

2019: the response referenced two AIRA guides and gave direction on approach; details not recorded.

2024: The purist view is to reject when they disagree, but IIS that AIRA has spoken with make the same vaccine-group-based decisions (reject when in different vaccine groups, accept when in the same group); changing all provider/EHR behavior would be an endless effort, with concern over lost records that might never be resubmitted.   • RXA-3 (date of administration)
  • RXA-5 (CVX)
  • RXA-9 (administered or historical indicator)
  • RXA-11.4 (administered-at location)

RXA-21 Action Code

How should an IIS handle Add, Update and Delete in RXA-21? Should an Update from the same provider be treated as a full replace (e.g., to correct a lot number or expiration date) instead of requiring a delete followed by an add, given that many administration systems cannot send deletes?

Asked 2× · 2018–2022 · VXU · Answered · Fields: RXA-21 · Related: Patient Matching & Deduplication

Answer given

Most IIS ignore the difference between an Add and an Update in RXA-21 (2018). They use vaccination matching and business rules to decide whether the incoming record is new or updates an existing one. Deletes are honored.

The 2022 answer elaborated:

  • Why codes cannot be trusted alone: This area needs standardization across IIS and administration systems. It is not as simple as Add/Update/Delete, because an IIS can receive the same vaccination event from more than one provider. An IIS cannot blindly trust an Add without checking for existing records, and the same is true for an Update.
  • MIROW guidance: Two chapters are relevant: vaccination matching and deduplication, and record consolidation when a match is found (selectively consolidating two records into one). Most IIS use the Add/Update codes loosely, mainly to know the message is not a delete. They apply MIROW-style logic to improve the details of the vaccination event rather than replacing or duplicating it wholesale.
  • Full replace: If Update were to mean full replace, the IIS would have to consider when a full replace is allowed. Many systems, including IIS, have difficulty updating records when key fields such as CVX or vaccination date change. Standard practice is to send a delete and then an add, and many IIS ask partners to do this, which explains the Add-Delete-Add pattern. For IIS that support deletes, Delete then Add is a functional way to achieve a full replace, but not all administration systems support deletes.
  • No defined rules: The IG and national guidance provide no business rules that differentiate Add (A) from Update (U). The current HL7 standard gives a submitter no way to indicate a full vs. partial replace.
