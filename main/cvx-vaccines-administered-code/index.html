CVX is the CDC-maintained code system designed specifically for immunization documentation and interoperability [9]. It is the canonical vocabulary used by IIS to represent what vaccination a patient received, whether the dose was newly administered or recorded historically. CVX has served as the foundation of IIS immunization history management for more than three decades and remains central to evaluation, forecasting, and cross-jurisdictional exchange.

Unlike product-oriented code systems, CVX represents the clinical concept of a vaccination rather than its packaging or supply-chain details. CVX codes exist at multiple levels of specificity, ranging from codes that closely align with particular products to broader category-level or Unspecified Formulation codes used when exact product information is unavailable. This structure allows IIS to preserve clinically meaningful vaccination histories even when data originates from legacy systems, foreign sources, or incomplete documentation.

For IIS operations, CVX is required for any vaccination that participates in evaluation, forecasting, or query/response workflows. While submitting systems may send other code systems, IIS must internally maintain or derive a CVX representation so that vaccination records can be consistently interpreted and processed. CVX therefore functions as the common semantic layer that enables consolidation of records across time, systems, and jurisdictions.

The level of CVX specificity directly affects data quality. More specific CVX codes support more accurate evaluation, forecasting, analytics, and reporting. Broader or Unspecified Formulation codes are sometimes necessary-particularly for historical or international records-but provide less precision for downstream use. Appropriate use of CVX specificity is therefore a shared responsibility between submitting systems and IIS.

See “Vaccinations Returned in Query Responses” for guidance on how IIS may normalize submitted CVX codes and what to expect in RSPs.

Conformance statements

  1. SHALLIIS and submitting systems SHALL use CVX as the canonical representation of administered and historical vaccinations for IIS operations, evaluation, and forecasting.s1
  2. SHALLIIS SHALL be able to receive CVX codes in VXU messages and SHALL be able to return CVX codes in RSP messages for all vaccinations maintained in the registry.⚑s2
  3. SHALLIIS SHALL internally maintain or derive a CVX value for each vaccination that participates in evaluation, forecasting, or query/response, regardless of which code system was originally submitted.s3
  4. SHALLSubmitting systems SHALL send the most specific CVX code available that accurately reflects the vaccine administered or historically received.s4
  5. SHOULDSubmitting systems SHOULD send a CVX code for every administered and historical vaccination, even when other code systems (e.g., NDC) are also sent.s5
  6. SHALLIIS SHALL accept CVX codes for historical vaccinations that represent valid doses administered at any time or location, including those administered outside the United States.s6
