2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication

IIS are designed to maintain a single, consolidated immunization history for each patient within their jurisdiction. To achieve this, IIS routinely perform patient and vaccination deduplication as part of normal operations. Submitters do not need to determine whether a patient or vaccination already exists in the IIS before sending data. IIS are explicitly built to receive overlapping, repeated, and partial information from many sources and reconcile it into a unified record.

When a VXU is received, the IIS evaluates whether the patient represented in the message corresponds to an existing patient or represents a new individual. If the patient is already known, the IIS associates the submitted vaccination with the correct patient record. IIS are similarly prepared to receive reports of vaccinations that were administered or recorded elsewhere. This includes historical vaccinations that may already exist in the system.

Deduplication and record consolidation are not limited to data submitted by different organizations. IIS may identify duplicate patients or vaccination events within submissions from the same provider, including duplicates that occur within a single data feed. Many duplicates are detected and resolved immediately during message processing. Others may be identified later through background processes designed to continuously improve data quality.

To make these determinations, IIS evaluate a combination of patient attributes and vaccination attributes. Patient attributes include name, date of birth, identifiers, and contact information. Vaccination attributes include administration date, vaccine product, lot number, and administration details. Records that meet high-confidence matching criteria may be merged automatically. More ambiguous cases may be queued for manual review according to IIS policy.

Submitters should be aware that patient and vaccination records they submit may be modified by the IIS as additional information is received from other sources, or as duplicate records are identified and consolidated over time. This behavior is expected and essential to the IIS mission of producing accurate, longitudinal immunization histories, rather than preserving individual submissions exactly as received.
