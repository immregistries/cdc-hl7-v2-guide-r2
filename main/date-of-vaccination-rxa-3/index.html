RXA-3 records when a vaccination was administered and distinguishes one vaccination event from another. The administration date is foundational for clinical history, deduplication, patient access, and forecasting logic. Time-of-day precision is not clinically relevant for immunization evaluation and must not distort the administration date.

Program measures for timely reporting of vaccinations are typically calculated using calendar-day differences rather than elapsed time based on timestamps. For example, a vaccination administered on one calendar day and reported on the following calendar day is considered reported "within one day," even when precise timestamps are unavailable. This confirms that the date-only representation in RXA-3 is intentional and does not conflict with timeliness expectations used in operational reporting programs.

Conformance statements

  1. SHALLRXA-3 SHALL represent the calendar date on which the vaccination was administered.⚑s1
  2. SHALLRXA-3 SHALL reflect the local date of administration in the time zone where the vaccination occurred.⚑s2
  3. SHALLTime component is not expected in typical operations but if it is included, RXA-3 SHALL use the local time zone of administration.⚑s3
  4. SHALL NOTSubmitting systems SHALL NOT report billing dates or processing dates in RXA-3.s4
  5. SHALLRXA-3 SHALL be accurate enough to support deduplication and patient-facing immunization records, even when time-of-day is not known.⚑s5
