2.8.2 Date of Birth (PID-7)

Quality review 1 warning · 7 notes

Findings from automated and editorial checks, for the authors and reviewers. Not part of the guide.

  • Warning Statement s3 The text allows a time-zone offset in PID-7; the profiles reject one. PID-7 uses TS_NZ, whose time pattern (DTM_NZ) has no offset, so a birth date/time with an offset fails format validation. The narrative tells senders to use the birth time zone and tells IIS to ignore any offset present. RXA-3 and OBX-14 also use TS_NZ. Their wording ("use the local time zone") is consistent if it means local time without an offset.
    ConflictAction: Narrative and IGAMT
    Narrative "if it is included, PID-7 SHALL be encoded using the time zone where the birth occurred." "When time or time-zone components are present in PID-7, receiving IIS SHALL ignore the time and offset components …"
    IGAMT export
    • PID_IZ01/PID_IZ03 PID-7: R [1..1] TS_NZ; DTM_NZ pattern YYYYMMDD[HH[MM[SS[.S[S[S[S]]]]]]] (no offset).
    • Z42 PID_IZ02 PID-7 uses TS_IZ01 instead.
    Recommendation Say "local time of birth, without a time-zone offset" in the narrative. If offsets must be tolerated, loosen the flavor. Align Z42 with the other profiles.

  • Note 5 requirements were written as paragraphs in the draft and are shown here as conformance statements (wording unchanged): s1, s2, s3, s4, s5.qf-statement-from-paragraph-bf1cb67a

PID-7 establishes a stable and authoritative patient date of birth used for identity matching across systems and jurisdictions. Preserving the correct calendar date of birth is critical for patient matching and must take precedence over technical time normalization or system defaults.

Unlike operational timestamps such as MSH-7, where time-zone interpretation is required, PID-7 is treated as a date value representing the patient's birth date in the local context of the birth event, not as a timestamp subject to time-zone conversion. Preserving the calendar date exactly as submitted, rather than converting it, avoids unintended date shifts that could otherwise occur if a receiving system attempts to normalize the value to its own local time zone. This distinction reduces the risk of date shifts during processing and helps maintain consistent patient matching across systems.

Conformance statements

  1. SHALLPID-7 SHALL represent the patient's official date of birth as recorded at the place and time of birth.⚑s1
  2. SHALLSubmitting systems SHALL use the date of birth recorded on official documentation when available.s2
  3. SHALLTime component is not expected in typical operations but if it is included, PID-7 SHALL be encoded using the time zone where the birth occurred.⚑s3
  4. SHALLWhen time or time-zone components are present in PID-7, receiving IIS SHALL ignore the time and offset components and SHALL preserve the submitted calendar date exactly as provided, without applying time-zone normalization.⚑s4
  5. SHALLPID-7 SHALL represent a single, consistent date of birth for the patient across submissions.⚑s5
