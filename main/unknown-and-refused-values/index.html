The value sets are open, so a validator would probably warn rather than fail.
    ConflictAction: CDC decision

    Recommendation Decide which coding system identifier these codes carry (the TAB says CDCREC), state it in 4.5.5.2, and make the IGAMT value sets match.

Multiple conventions exist for representing unknown race and ethnicity values. To support interoperability across IIS communities with different historical practices, CDCREC may be extended for community needs; this guide adopts the community-accepted unknown codes: UNK, ASKU, and PHC1175. See the Additional "Unknown" Race and Ethnicity Codes TAB [8] and Section 7.4, Value Sets, for the IG-maintained authoritative lists.

While ASKU ("Asked but unknown") and PHC1175 ("Refused to answer") convey slightly different intent, IIS are not required to preserve this distinction operationally. Patients are not obligated to provide race or ethnicity, and programmatic requirements to populate these fields do not override national interoperability expectations.

Conformance statements

  1. MAYSending systems MAY use UNK, ASKU, or PHC1175 when race or ethnicity is unknown or not provided.s1
  2. SHOULDSending systems SHOULD select and consistently implement either ASKU or PHC1175 as their preferred "unknown" code, but not both.⚑s2
  3. MAYSending systems MAY use UNK where a general unknown designation is appropriate.s3
  4. SHALLIIS SHALL accept UNK, ASKU, and PHC1175.⚑s4
  5. MAYIIS MAY translate ASKU or PHC1175 to UNK or to an empty value according to local policy.s5
  6. SHALL NOTIIS SHALL NOT reject messages because these codes are present.s6
  7. SHALL NOTIIS SHALL NOT define additional local "unknown" codes.⚑s7
