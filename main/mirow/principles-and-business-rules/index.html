Summary

The guide doesn't cite this report, but it is the easiest way to find a MIROW rule by number. It also brings in two MIROW guides that this collection doesn't otherwise include, and both connect to the guide. The 2011 VFC collaboration guide is where per-dose eligibility comes from: one eligibility status for each dose, recorded after the dose is given, and updated later if an insurance claim shows the first answer was wrong. The guide follows all of this. It does not give MIROW's rule for choosing one eligibility code when a patient qualifies more than one way (Medicaid, then AI/AN, then uninsured, then underinsured), although it allows only one value. It also does not mention that MIROW's best practice is to report a combination. The 2009 reminder/recall guide makes a patient's reminder/recall preference, including opting out in whole or in part, a criterion for every reminder/recall run. That preference travels in PD1-11 (publicity code, RE, with twelve national codes). The guide never mentions PD1-11, so submitters have no guidance on a field the profile expects them to send when known.

All principles and business rules from the ten MIROW guides, with their current wording, numbered by guide and year, with examples and remarks, and with terms from the MIROW Common Vocabulary marked.		Missing Note

The guide cites individual MIROW guides but not the compiled rules, which are the stable way to point to a MIROW rule. The ERR-5 code set cites MIROW rules by bare number (BR 116) without the guide, which is how its citations became ambiguous (see mirow-dqa-45).

Recommendation: Cite the MIROW Principles and Business Rules report among the references. Ask AIRA to cite rules in the ERR-5 code set by their full name (for example, DQA2022 - BR115).

2.6 Data Quality Processes, 4.6.4.3 IIS Matching and Correction Processing

DLE2011 - BR608, BR613 - One eligibility status for every dose administered

A single eligibility or coverage status is assigned to the patient for every vaccination event, and reported to the IIS for every dose. On the same day a patient can be eligible for one VFC vaccine and not for another.		Covered

The guide's per-dose eligibility, at most one value per dose, is this rule. The guide also says eligibility may vary by vaccine.

4.6.5.1 s1, 4.6.5.1 s3, 4.6.5 Funding Source and Eligibility

DLE2011 - BR614, BR615 - Which eligibility to report when several apply

The minimum is a single item: Medicaid, AI/AN, uninsured, underinsured (at FQHC, RHC, or deputized providers only), grantee-eligible, or private coverage. When the patient meets more than one VFC criterion, the hierarchy is Medicaid, then AI/AN, then uninsured, then underinsured. The best practice is to report every applicable item in a valid combination (for example, AI/AN and uninsured), with rules for which combinations are valid and how to interpret them.		Conflict Note

The national value set (V01 to V05 and V22 to V25) has one code per category, and the guide allows at most one value per dose. That supports MIROW's minimum set but not its best practice. The guide does not give the hierarchy for choosing among codes, so a patient who is both on Medicaid and AI/AN may be reported either way.

Recommendation: CDC to confirm that only one eligibility value is sent per dose, which departs from MIROW's best practice (DLE2011 BR615). If so, add to Patient Funding Eligibility (Per Dose) that when more than one applies, the submitter reports the first in MIROW's order: V02 (Medicaid), V04 (AI/AN), V03 (uninsured), V05 (underinsured).

Action: CDC decision

4.6.5.1 s3, 4.6.5.3 Jurisdiction-Specific Eligibility Codes

DLE2011 - BR610 - Eligibility later found to be wrong

If private coverage was assumed at the encounter and the claim is later rejected, the provider organization updates the patient's eligibility in the IIS to reflect reality and deals with borrowing. It must not change which vaccine (lot) was given.		Covered

Eligibility is not an identifying field, so the correction is an update to the same record (same ORC-3), which is what MIROW describes.

4.6.4.2 s3, 4.6.5.4 s3, 3.2.5.3 Modifying Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields

DLE2011 - BR612 - Record after administration

Record the vaccination event only after the vaccine has been administered, so the data reflect what actually happened (the vaccine could be declined).		Covered

The same rule as DINV2016 BR201 (see mirow-di-08); a declined dose is reported as non-administration.

4.6.1.1 s1, 4.8.1.2 Dose-Level Non-Administration

DLE2011 - BR601, BR605 - Age and provider type limits on eligibility

A patient must meet the program's age criteria (under 19 for VFC). Underinsured children are VFC-eligible only at an FQHC, RHC, or a provider with delegated authority.		Covered

The value set's V05 is limited to FQHC, RHC, and deputized providers. An inconsistent combination is accepted and flagged rather than rejected, with ERR-5 code 2012 (eligibility conflicts with other data, such as age).

4.6.5.4 Validation, Enforcement, and "Borrowing" Scenarios, 4.6.5.2 s2, 3.3.3.4 ERR-5: Application Error Code

RR2009 - BR401 - Who is included in reminder/recall, including opt-out

Criteria for including people in reminder/recall include age, the provider association (immunization home), patient status at both levels, vaccines and doses due, risk status, address, program, insurance, exemptions and contraindications, language, and opting out of reminder/recall in whole or in part. The CDC-endorsed "reminder/ recall status" (publicity code) records whether a person wishes to be contacted.		Missing Warning

PD1-11 (publicity code) carries the patient's reminder/recall preference. It is RE in the VXU, with twelve required national codes for what is allowed: none, reminder or recall only, by any method or no calls, or to the provider. PD1-18 carries its date. The narrative never mentions PD1-11. Submitters can't tell what the codes mean, that the field is separate from PD1-12 (sharing) and from patient status, or that an empty field means no preference was recorded. MIROW keeps opting out of reminder/recall separate from patient status (MPS2019 P313, P314; see mirow-mps-11).

Recommendation: Add a short section on PD1-11 to Patient Information. Say that it records the patient's or guardian's explicit reminder/recall preference, with the date in PD1-18. Say that it is sent only when a preference was expressed, that it is independent of PD1-12 and of patient status, and that IIS use it to filter reminder/recall (MIROW RR2009 BR401).

Action: Narrative

4.5 Patient Information, 4.5.6 Consent and Privacy, 4.5.7.2 Active and Inactive Status

RR2009 - P201 to P204, BR201, BR202, BR802, P803 - Who is responsible for reminder/recall

Responsibility for each patient must be clearly defined. The immunization home does reminder/recall when it is known, and the jurisdiction does it otherwise. After 90 days and three unsuccessful attempts, the patient becomes inactive at the provider level and stays active at the jurisdiction level, so responsibility passes to the jurisdiction.		Covered

The guide's patient status sections explain that status supports reminder/recall and that IIS derive their own status, which they keep out of messages. A status the IIS sets after failed recalls is IIS-derived (see mirow-mps-07 on telling providers about changes).

4.5.7 Patient Active Status in Clinical and IIS Contexts, 4.5.7.4 IIS-Derived Status and Jurisdictional Views

RR2009 - P603, P702 - Reminder/recall depends on data quality

The notification method depends on the contact information available (a phone call needs a current phone number), and the specificity of the notification depends on the quality of the immunization data.		Covered

The guide's phone and email section says accurate contact information supports reminder/recall.

4.5.4 Phone and Email, 2.6.2 Shared Responsibility for Data Quality

DQA2013 - BR818 to BR823 - Organization acquisitions, mergers, and spin-offs

When IIS-authorized organizations merge, are acquired, or split, the IIS de-authorizes or restructures them and assigns new IIS-AO IDs according to the case.		Covered

The guide asks IIS to document how identifiers are requested, updated, and retired, and submitters to tell the IIS about changes in ownership or structure. MIROW's rules are what an IIS would follow.

2.7.8 Implementation and Maintenance Considerations, 2.7.6 s1

BC2019 - Business continuity planning

Principles and business rules for keeping IIS functions running through a disruption (recovery objectives, essential functions, communication).		Out of scope

Nothing in these rules depends on message content. Fault handling at the transport level is the guide's nearest topic.
