Immunization Information System Inventory Management Operations

Best practices for vaccine inventory management in an IIS. Less tied to messaging than the decrementing inventory guide that followed it.

Summary

This 2012 guide is mostly about inventory operations inside the IIS. Its connection to the guide is the dose-administered transaction, which is the only inventory event an EHR reports electronically. For that transaction the two agree: - a lot number and dose-level eligibility for every administered dose - a funding source reported for the dose itself, rather than inferred from eligibility, so that borrowing can be seen - no inventory transaction for a historical dose - an IIS that keeps the vaccination of an opted-out patient out of the record but can still account for the dose The guide is softer than this MIROW guide in one way. MIROW 2012 says an administered dose reported without a lot number or eligibility is rejected. The guide follows the later MIROW guides, which record the vaccination and deal with the missing data separately. Two connections need attention. MIROW bases inventory on the NDC and lot number on the outside of the package, and says a multi-component vaccine has one lot number and one NDC for inventory. The guide asks submitters for the NDC of the lyophilized powder and lets RXA-15 repeat without saying what each lot number means. MIROW also distinguishes the organization responsible for the inventory from the one that gave the dose. The guide's definition of RXA-11 assumes they are the same.

Where the guide connects

The best practice saysResultIn the guide

Chapter 2 - Scope; BR718 - Electronic inventory transactions p. 17-23, 46-47

Dose-administered transactions reach the IIS by direct entry or from an EHR. Shipments come from VTrckS. All other inventory transactions (wasted, spoiled, transferred, returned doses) are entered only through the IIS user interface, because "there are no electronic exchange standards" for them, though MIROW would encourage their development.		Out of scope

The guide covers vaccinations, not inventory transactions, and its scope excludes inventory ordering. Reporting wasted or transferred doses electronically is still not defined anywhere in the national guidance.

1.1.2 Scope

P701, BR701, BR702, chapter 7 - NDC supremacy, and finding the NDC from the lot number p. 36-37, 83-86

Inventory is managed by NDC, because VFC and VTrckS require it. The NDC used is the one received in the shipment file, from the outside of the package (unit of sale). Providers rarely record the NDC when they give a dose. The recommended way for the IIS to find the NDC of an administered dose is from its lot number, which was linked to an NDC when the shipment arrived. Mapping from CVX and MVX without the lot number is not a best practice, and asking providers to record the NDC by hand is not suggested.		Reworded Note

The guide keeps CVX as the canonical code and treats NDC as a SHOULD for administered doses, valuable for traceability and reconciliation. That fits MIROW's view that the IIS can derive the NDC behind the scenes. The guide accepts unit-of-use as well as unit-of-sale NDCs. An IIS that receives a unit-of-use NDC (the vial or syringe) still has to reach the unit-of-sale NDC for inventory, which MIROW does through the lot number. The guide doesn't say this, but it is IIS processing.

4.6.2.3 NDC - National Drug Code, 4.6.2.3.3 Relationship Between NDC and CVX, 4.6.1.8.2 s1, 4.6.2.3.1 Unit-of-Use and Unit-of-Sale NDCs, 4.6.2.4.3 s3, 4.6.2.3.4 Storage and Traceability Expectations

BR704 (remarks), chapter 7 - Multi-component vaccines p. 38, 85

Each component of a multi-component vaccine (one with a diluent or components that are combined) has its own lot number. There is always one lot number and one NDC, on the outside of the package, that characterize the vaccine as a whole, and those are used for inventory. For a product such as Pentacel, with separate NDCs for powder and liquid, the only NDC that can be used for ordering, inventory, and returns is the one on the CDC contract.		Partly covered Warning

The guide tells submitters to report the NDC of the lyophilized powder for reconstituted vaccines, and any NDC for other multi-component products. It says nothing about which lot number goes in RXA-15. RXA-15 repeats (1..*) in the administered RXA, so a submitter could send every component's lot, the outer package's, or one chosen at random. MIROW's answer is one lot number and one NDC from the outside of the package. The guide's NDC rule picks the powder, which serves product identification but not inventory. The same RXA-15 gap was found in the SISC and consolidation comparisons (sisc-lot-01, mirow-cr-08).

Recommendation: CDC to decide which lot number a submitter sends in RXA-15 for a vaccine with several components: the one on the outer package that the IIS inventory knows (MIROW BR704), or the active component's, to match the NDC rule. Say so with the RXA-15 cardinality fix.

Action: CDC decision

4.6.2.4.3 s9, 4.6.2.4.3 s10, 4.6.2.4.3 s11, 3.2.4.2 Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields

GR703 - 5-4-2 NDC format p. 53, 84

IIS should use the 5-4-2 NDC format that VTrckS requires, and translate the other formats (4-4-2, 5-3-2, 5-4-1, and formats with fillers) into it.		Covered

Submitting systems SHALL support NDC11 in 5-4-2 with dashes, and IIS SHALL accept common variations.

4.6.2.3.2 NDC Formatting Expectations, 4.6.2.4.3 s4, 4.6.2.4.3 s8

P702, BR704 - A lot number for every administered dose p. 38

Every administered dose must be documented and reported with its lot number, and a report without one is rejected. Historical doses do not need one. Providers should adopt barcoding and drop-down lists so the lot number is recorded correctly.		Reworded Note

RXA-15 is required in the administered RXA and optional in the historical RXA, which matches MIROW. But the guide does not let an IIS reject a vaccination only because the lot number is missing, unless the IIS has declared it Critical Data. The later MIROW guides take the same position: the 2016 decrementing guide records the vaccination and skips decrementing (S704), and the 2022 data quality guide makes the lot number recommended (BR131). So MIROW's 2012 rejection rule has been superseded.

4.6.1.1 s2, 3.3.1 IIS Core Intent and Message Disposition, 3.3.5 s2

BR705 - Patient eligibility for every administered dose p. 39

Patient eligibility must be documented and reported for every administered dose, and a report without it is rejected. Eligibility is needed to know whether to decrement public or private stock when both hold the same lot. It does not apply to historical doses.		Reworded Note

Eligibility is reported for each administered dose. A jurisdiction may enforce it for participants in public programs, but the guide prefers accepting the vaccination and following up. The 2016 decrementing guide (S701) and ERR-5 code 2500 take the same approach.

4.6.5.1 s1, 4.6.5.2 s4, 4.6.5.6 s2

BR706 - Organization responsible for the inventory p. 39-40

The provider organization responsible for the inventory must be associated with every dose administered. It may differ from the organization that gave the vaccine, for example when a central depot redistributes vaccine. A report without it can still be used for inventory if the IIS can determine the responsible organization from what was submitted (that is, not an umbrella organization).		Partly covered Note

The guide defines the administering organization in RXA-11 as "the entity that physically administered the vaccine and managed its inventory", which assumes the two are the same. MSH-22 identifies the organization responsible for the data, not the inventory. Where they differ, nothing in the message says whose inventory to decrement. MIROW accepts that the IIS works this out from what it knows of the organizations.

Recommendation: In Administering Organization (RXA-11), drop "and managed its inventory" or qualify it. Note that the inventory may belong to another organization (MIROW BR706), which the IIS determines from its records of the submitter's organizations rather than from the message.

2.7.5 Administering Organization (RXA-11), 2.7.3 Responsible Organization (MSH-22), 2.7.6 Parent and Child Organizations

P708, BR707, BR711, BR725, chapter 8 - Borrowing and the public/private indicator p. 40-43, 49-50, 87-88

Borrowing between public and private stock should be rare, tracked at the dose level, and repaid. The best practice is a separate public/private indicator for each dose. Inferring public or private from eligibility alone is not recommended, because it hides mismatches between eligibility and the stock used.		Covered

The guide asks submitters to report both eligibility and funding source, as they actually applied, including in borrowing. That is the best practice MIROW recommends and the 2016 decrementing guide adopted (mirow-di-05).

4.6.5 Funding Source and Eligibility, 4.6.5.2 Vaccine Funding Source and Stock Models, 4.6.5.4 s3

BR711 (remarks) - Lot number field used for other purposes p. 42-43

Some EHRs extend the lot number field to mark public or private stock. The IIS needs to separate the real lot number from the public/private designation and record them in two fields.		Partly covered Note

Now that funding source has its own OBX, there is no reason to put it in RXA-15. The guide doesn't say that RXA-15 carries only the lot number, as the 2022 data quality guide asks (see mirow-dqa-24).

Recommendation: Covered by the recommendation in mirow-dqa-24. When adding it, say that stock designations belong in the funding source OBX, not in RXA-15.

Action: Narrative

3.2.4.2 Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields, 4.6.5 Funding Source and Eligibility

BR711, BR712, BR718 - Expiration date and short-dated lots p. 42-43, 46-47

For each dose, the lot number expiration date is recorded and reported along with the lot number. When a lot is short-dated, the new expiration date is used. The same lot number can then exist as two inventory items with different expiration dates, so the lot number alone may match more than one item, and the IIS will not decrement when it cannot find a unique one.		Covered

RXA-16 is RE in the administered RXA and can be corrected by update. The narrative does not explain its role in matching inventory (see mirow-di-03).

3.2.4.2 Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields

BR711 (remarks), chapter 7 - Half and double doses p. 42-43, 84

When a provider gives two pediatric doses for an adult dose, or half an adult dose to a child, the IIS can use a dose size (half, full, double) to decrement the right amount, or decrement the extra by hand.		Covered

RXA-6 (administered amount) and RXA-7 (units) carry the volume actually given, which lets an IIS detect a half or double dose. The guide does not discuss the case, which is a local inventory practice.

3.2.4.2 Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields

BR716 - Event date and recording date p. 45

Both the date an inventory event occurred and the date it was recorded should be kept. For example, a dose may need to be re-designated as VFC three months later when an insurance claim is denied.		Covered

RXA-3 carries the date of administration and MSH-7 the date of the message. A later change of eligibility or funding source is sent as an update to the same record.

2.8.3 s1, 2.8.1 Message Header Date/Time (MSH-7), 3.2.5.3 Modifying Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields

BR718, PM07 - Flag doses that do not match the inventory p. 46-47, 129

When a dose reported by an EHR cannot be matched to a lot in the provider's inventory, the IIS must flag the discrepancy to the provider organization, preferably immediately and in any case before the reconciliation period closes. The submitter of HL7 data should have good visibility of reported vaccinations that did not match known lots, and a simple process to resolve them.		Partly covered Note

The guide's ACK rules support this, but the guide does not say that an unmatched lot is reported. This is the same gap as mirow-di-12.

Recommendation: Covered by the recommendation in mirow-di-12, which should cite MIROW 2012 BR718 as well as the 2016 guide.

4.6.4.5 Inventory and Accountability Considerations, 3.3.3.2 s6, 3.3.3.3 Required Follow-Up Actions

BR719, BR720, P707 - Opted-out patients p. 47-48

For a vaccination submitted by an EHR for a patient who has opted out of the IIS, the IIS should decrement inventory without updating the patient record. Some IIS instead reject the submission and the provider decrements by hand.		Covered

In opt-out jurisdictions the guide has submitters send all records and leaves opt-out enforcement to the IIS. That is what lets the IIS decrement the dose without keeping the vaccination, as BR720 recommends.

4.5.6.2 s1, 4.5.6 Consent and Privacy

Chapter 2, BR704 - Historical doses do not affect inventory p. 21, 38

Historical immunizations sent by an EHR should not create an inventory transaction, since they do not affect the provider's current physical inventory.		Covered4.6.5.6 s2, 4.6.1.1 s3

P704, GR704 - Inventory transactions are reversible p. 44, 53

Any inventory transaction should be reversible and correctable, and the IIS should support corrections to existing inventory transactions.		Covered

The guide's update and delete model gives the submitter a way to correct or retract a dose. The IIS makes the matching inventory adjustment.
