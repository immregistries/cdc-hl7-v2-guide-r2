Immunization Information Systems Patient-Level De-duplication Best Practices

CDC's best practices for finding and merging duplicate patient records in an IIS, the patient-level counterpart to the MIROW vaccination-level deduplication guide.

Copy in this repository · Repository page

Not a MIROW guide; kept here because it is the patient-level companion to the MIROW deduplication guidance. The repository links to a CDC address that no longer works (https://www.cdc.gov/vaccines/programs/iis/interop-proj/downloads/de-duplication.pdf); this copy is from the Internet Archive.

Summary

The guide does not cite this document, although it is the reference for the patient deduplication that the guide tells submitters to expect. The two agree: - IIS clean up and standardize the data they receive. - Identifiers such as a medical record number allow a quick, reliable lookup. - Onboarding includes systematic testing of the content of incoming data. - Submitters should act on the IIS's error feedback. - Data from an unapproved source is not accepted. The guide also does something this report asked for: in 2013 CDC suggested that placeholder names for newborns ("Baby Boy", "BG") "could be standardized by convention on a national scale". The guide now marks them with name type NB and requires the real name to be sent once known. Two earlier findings apply here as well. The guide doesn't say that IIS deliberately prefer an unmerged duplicate to a wrong merge (mirow-vd-04). It also never mentions birth order, which this report lists among the optional matching fields and MIROW uses to keep twins apart (mirow-cr-09).

2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication, 4.6.4.3 IIS Matching and Correction Processing

Table 1.1, 5.2.1 (6) - Err on the side of preventing false merges p. 4, 53

When in doubt, practitioners should prevent false merges. Wrongly merging two patients' records has more severe consequences than leaving one patient duplicated.		Reworded Note

The guide says high-confidence matches may be merged automatically and ambiguous cases reviewed, but not that a duplicate is the preferred error. The same point was made for vaccination events (see mirow-vd-04, whose recommendation should apply to patients as well).

2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication

4.1.2, 4.3.1, 5.2.1 (5) - Placeholder names for newborns p. 32, 34, 53

Nearly all interface data needs pre-screening. IIS may decide not to use records with names like Baby Boy, Baby Girl, BB, or BG, and should remove placeholder words before matching. In the longer term, "placeholder information could be standardized by convention on a national scale".		Guide adds Note

The guide supplies the national convention. A placeholder name is marked with name type 'NB' and never sent as the legal name. When the real name is known, the submitter sends both names together, along with the child's complete history, so the IIS can link the records.

4.5.2.7 Unnamed Newborns, 3.2.5.7 Official Registry Name Recorded for a Newborn, 4.5.2.3 Important Names

3.6.2, Table 1.1 - Use unique identifiers for a quick match p. 20

Where available, specific identifiers (SSN, medical record number, chart number, birth certificate number) can find a match quickly without calling the deduplication engine.		Covered

The guide makes the submitter's identifier with its assigning authority, and the IIS identifier, the national minimum. It leaves other identifiers, such as SSN, to jurisdictional policy because of their privacy risk.

4.5.1.1 Primary Patient Identifier for Exchange, 4.5.1.2 Assigning Authority and Identifier Namespace, 4.5.1.10 Other Patient Identifiers, 4.5.1.7 IIS Registry Identifier

3.6.1 - Fields used for matching p. 19-20

Most IIS match on name, date of birth, and gender. Mother's maiden name, address, and vaccination data are common. Optional fields that help include SSN, birth order, race, ethnicity, and local record numbers.		Partly covered Note

The guide covers names, date of birth, address, race, and ethnicity in detail. It doesn't discuss the multiple birth indicator or birth order (PID-24, PID-25), which this report lists as an optional matching field and MIROW's consolidation guide relies on to tell twins apart.

Recommendation: Covered by the recommendation in mirow-cr-09 to add a short section on multiple births.

4.5 Patient Information, 4.5.2 Patient and Other Names, 4.5.5 Race and Ethnicity, 2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication

3.5, 4.3 - Data preparation and standardization p. 17-18, 33-38

Data from many sources differs in conventions, abbreviations, and errors. IIS should standardize names (removing placeholders and titles), addresses, and phone numbers before comparing them, and require a minimal set of data for matching.		Covered

The guide expects IIS to cleanse and normalize what they receive, and tells submitters that the names and addresses returned may differ from what they sent.

4.5.3.6 Address Cleansing and Normalization, 4.5.2 Patient and Other Names, 4.5.4 Phone and Email, 4.5.2.10 Name Consistency and Matching Expectations

Table 1.1, 4.1.2, 5.2.1 - Onboarding, feedback, and approved sources p. 6-7, 32, 51-53

Test the format and content of incoming data systematically when onboarding a new data source. Give providers routine feedback on data quality, and encourage them to act on response files and error messages and to use standardized HL7. Do not accept data from a source that is not approved.		Covered

Connections are authenticated and tied to an onboarded organization in MSH-4. Onboarding and ongoing monitoring are part of conformance. Submitters must show users every Warning and Error, and analyze ACKs in aggregate.

2.7.2 s2, 5.5 Operational Enforcement and Ongoing Compliance, 3.3.3.3 Required Follow-Up Actions, 3.3.5 s3

Chapters 3 and 4 - The deduplication process inside the IIS p. 13-49

Data preparation, lookup by identifiers, blocking and scoring, action on outcomes, and reporting. Deterministic and probabilistic methods, thresholds, performance measures, retrospective review, merge and unmerge, and master patient indexes.		Out of scope

IIS processing. The guide describes only what submitters can expect from it: that their records may be merged and modified as the IIS consolidates them.
