Data Quality Assurance in Immunization Information Systems

Best practices for assuring the quality of data sent to and held in an IIS, across onboarding, incoming data, and data at rest, with the principles and business rules that support them.

Summary

The guide and the 2022 MIROW data quality guide agree on the approach: data quality is a continuous process shared by submitters and IIS, not something conformance rules or message rejection can deliver; a message should report what actually happened even when it falls short; and IIS should accept and flag rather than reject. Most of the MIROW business rules that check message content (date conflicts, expired lots, age ranges, route and site, license dates, unspecified formulations) are not conformance rules in the guide but have national ERR-5 codes, which is the right connection. The gaps are mostly things MIROW says about message content that the guide does not yet say: how to report a date whose day is unknown, that a combination vaccine is one vaccination event, what RXA-15 may contain, how an unknown dose amount is sent, and which identifier goes with a provider's name. Two clean-ups are needed: the ERR-5 code set still cites the business rule numbers of the retired 2008 and 2013 guides (and one code keeps a 14-day rule that MIROW replaced with 24 hours), and the guide cites this MIROW guide under a title it does not have.

2.6.2 Shared Responsibility for Data Quality, 2.6 Data Quality Processes, 2.5.5 Data-Quality Management Beyond ACKs

P10 - Accurately reflect vaccination event

A vaccination event submission should accurately reflect the vaccination event that actually occurred. Even if it does not meet data quality standards (for example, an unusual site for the vaccine), it is accurate if it reflects what happened.		Covered

The guide says the same for funding and eligibility ("report ... what actually applied") and in general.

4.6.5.4 s3, 2.5.5 Data-Quality Management Beyond ACKs

P05 - Mandatory data elements; P08 - Submit all available information

Submissions should contain the minimum/mandatory data elements to be accepted, and IIS-AOs should collect and submit as much information as possible ("we do not want minimum data, we want good data").		Covered

The guide's reading of RE (populate whenever the information is known) is the message-level form of P08.

2.6.1 Interpretation of RE ("Required by may be Empty"), 2.9.1 Usage Designations and Expectations

BR001 - Minimum/mandatory data elements (BR001 table)

Every submission names the submitting organization, the patient's first and last names, and date of birth. An administered vaccination also names the vaccinating organization, and a historical one the recording organization; both have the vaccination event date, the vaccine type, and the administered/historical indicator. A demographic record from Vital Records also has the birth certificate number, birth facility, and gender.		Partly covered Warning

Most of the list is required in the guide (MSH-4, PID-5, PID-7, RXA-3, RXA-5, RXA-9, and RXA-11 for administered doses). Two elements differ. The recording organization, mandatory for a historical submission in MIROW, is ORC-17 in the guide, which is RE and which the guide says IIS "generally do not treat ... as a condition for message acceptance", adding that its usage may be reconsidered if adoption stays limited. The Vital Records elements (birth certificate number, birth facility) are not discussed; Vital Records feeds are outside the guide's main use cases.

Recommendation: CDC to decide whether the guide should expect ORC-17 for historical vaccinations, as MIROW does, or say why not. Either way, the ORC-17 section should note that MIROW treats the recording organization as mandatory for historical submissions.

Action: CDC decision

2.7.4 Entering (Recording) Organization (ORC-17), 4.2 CDC Core Data Elements for Functional Standards, 2.7.2 s1, 2.7.5 s1

BR101 - Authorized provider organization; BR105 - IIS-AO approved for EDE

An IIS should accept submissions only from authorized provider organizations, and accept electronic data exchange only from an IIS-AO approved for it.		Covered2.7.2 s2, 2.7.2 s3, 2.7.3 Responsible Organization (MSH-22)

BR173 to BR175 - IIS-AO IDs; P21 - Complete chain of submitting organizations

Each IIS-AO has a unique ID that is never reused and does not embed information that can change (relationships, location); submitting organizations should include valid IIS-AO IDs for all IIS-AOs involved; and a submission should identify the complete chain of submitting organizations.		Reworded Note

The guide makes the IIS the authority for unique organization identifiers and gives each role its own field (MSH-4 submitting, MSH-22 responsible, ORC-17 recording, RXA-11 administering). Its model of the "chain" is narrower than P21: one submitting organization per authenticated connection, with intermediaries such as HIEs transparent. That is a reasonable message-level reading of P21, but the guide does not say so, and it does not carry BR174's advice against embedding changeable information in identifiers.

Recommendation: In Implementation and Maintenance Considerations, add that identifiers should not embed information that can change, such as location or parent organization (MIROW BR174).

An IIS should record and act on the action code (RXA-21) of every vaccination event submission, supporting at least 'A' (add) and 'D' (delete).		Covered4.6.4 Updates and Deletes, 4.6.4.4 s1

BR109 - Standard value tables

BR110 - Valid calendar dates in a submission

A date in a submission should be a valid calendar date. Complete dates are best, but if the day of a date is not available, the 15th of the month can be submitted.		Missing Warning

The guide requires RXA-3 to be the calendar date of administration but does not say what to send when only the month and year of a historical dose are known, which is common with vaccine cards and patient recall.

Recommendation: CDC to decide how a historical vaccination with an unknown day is reported (the 15th of the month, following MIROW BR110, or a month-precision date), and say it in Historical Vaccinations or Date of Vaccination (RXA-3).

2.8.3 Date of Vaccination (RXA-3), 4.6.1.2 Historical Vaccinations

BR151 - Minimum date of birth; BR152 - Date of birth default

The guide says to use the date of birth from official documentation but not what to do when it is unknown. Note that MIROW's default for an unknown day is the 1st for a date of birth (BR152) but the 15th for other dates (BR110).

4.6.2.4.1 Accepted Code Systems for Administered Vaccinations, 4.6.2.2 CVX - Vaccines Administered Code

BR118 - Specified formulation for administered

An administered vaccination should not use an unspecified formulation CVX code.		Covered

The guide says SHOULD NOT, tells the IIS to accept rather than reject, and to monitor; ERR-5 code 2213 reports it.

4.6.2.2.3 s2, 4.6.2.2.3 s4, 4.6.2.2.3 s5

BR120 - Combination vaccine reported as single vaccination event

A combination vaccine dose should be reported as a single vaccination event (MMRV as one dose of MMRV, not a dose of MMR and a dose of varicella).		Missing Warning

The guide does not say this for U.S. combination vaccines. The only statement on combinations is the opposite case: a foreign combination product with no CVX code SHOULD be decomposed into its component antigens, one vaccination event each. Without the general rule, a reader could take the foreign-product rule as the norm.

Recommendation: In the CVX section, state that a combination vaccine is reported as one RXA with the combination's CVX (MIROW BR120), and make clear that decomposing into components applies only to foreign products with no CVX code.

Action: Narrative

4.6.2.4.1 s6, 3.3.3.4 ERR-5: Application Error Code

BR127 - Vaccination event dosage

An administered vaccination should have a dosage that is a positive number with a unit of volume (for example, mL). A value of 999 in HL7 means the amount was not filled in.		Partly covered Warning

RXA-6 and RXA-7 are required in the administered RXA flavor, but the narrative says nothing about either beyond listing dose volume as a non-identifying field, and does not say how to send an unknown amount. MIROW's remark relies on the Release 1.5 convention of 999.

4.6.1.1 s2, 3.2.4.2 Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields

BR129, BR130, BR132 - Lot number content (Appendix K)

A lot number should contain only letters, digits, and dashes (no spaces around a dash); hold a single lot number and nothing else; and not be prefixed, appended, or embedded with extraneous strings.		Missing Warning

The guide names lot number quality as a problem conformance cannot solve but says nothing about what RXA-15 should contain. RXA-15 may repeat in the administered flavor (for a unit-of-sale and a unit-of-use lot), which fits BR130 only if each repetition holds one lot number. Recommendation: Add to Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields (or the RXA-15 field notes) that each repetition of RXA-15 carries exactly one lot number as printed on the product, with no other text, and point to MIROW BR129-BR132 and Appendix K.

6.1.2 Support for Previous Versions

Comparison of past MIROW data quality best practices (published with the guide)

The 2022 guide renumbered the business rules of the retired 2008 and 2013 data quality guides; its companion comparison maps each old rule to its replacement or marks it retired. For example, old BR118 (date after lot expiration) is now BR115, BR119 (route and site) is BR128, and BR115 (submission within 14 days) became BR106 (within 24 hours).		Conflict Warning

The national ERR-5 code set cites MIROW rules by their old numbers. Seven codes cite numbers that now mean something else: 2001 (BR 118, now BR115), 2010 (BR 116, now BR126), 2016 and 2017 (BR 119, now BR128), 2018 (BR 101, now BR111), 2019 (BR 103, now BR113), and 2022 (BR102, now BR114). Three more cite numbers that do not match the code's content: 2214 cites BR 114 for the provider-type check (old BR113, now BR168), 2215 cites BR 130 for the license date range (old BR120, now BR133), and 2217 cites BR 113 for the patient name type. Code 2023 keeps the retired 14-day submission rule, which MIROW and this guide now put at 24 hours. Code 2024 cites a rule (medical contraindication) that MIROW retired.

Recommendation: Ask AIRA, which maintains the code set, to cite the 2022 rule numbers (written "DQA2022 BRnnn"), to align code 2023 with the 24-hour expectation, and to decide whether code 2024 is still needed.

Action: CDC decision
