Management of Patient Status in Immunization Information Systems

Best practices for recording a patient's status (active or inactive, and why) at the provider organization and jurisdiction levels, including the values exchanged in HL7 messages.

Published PDF · Copy in this repository · Repository page

Version 3.0 (March 2019), a repackaging and renaming of Management of Patient Active/Inactive Status in IIS (2015), which replaced Management of Moved or Gone Elsewhere Status and Other Patient Designations in IIS (2005). The file name of the published copy is dated June 2020.

Summary

The guide and this MIROW guide agree on the essentials: status at the provider organization level is the provider's statement about its own relationship with the patient, status at the jurisdiction level belongs to the IIS, deceased overrides everything, a vaccination is not refused for lack of a status, and opting out is separate from status. The guide is more skeptical of provider-reported status ("advisory rather than authoritative"), which fits current practice. The main gap is that the guide's patient status sections never name the fields: MIROW's chapter 7 maps each provider-level status to a PD1-16 code (with its date in PD1-17) and uses PID-29 and PID-30 for death, but the guide says none of this, nor that code 'P' (permanently inactive) is how MIROW reads a reported death. MIROW also asks for status in every submission and for the IIS to tell providers when it changes their patients' status, while the guide forbids sending IIS-derived status in messages; the guide should say how those fit.

The best practice saysResultIn the guide

P301, P302, P304 - Status levels and who assigns them

Each status describes the relationship between a patient and one party responsible for the patient's vaccinations: a provider organization (lower level) or a geographic jurisdiction (higher level). Provider-level status may be set by the provider organization or the immunization program; jurisdiction-level status only by the immunization program.		Covered4.5.7 Patient Active Status in Clinical and IIS Contexts, 4.5.7.1 Provider-Reported Status, 4.5.7.4 IIS-Derived Status and Jurisdictional Views

Chapter 7, Table 8 - Coding provider-level status in HL7 p. 55-56

Provider-level status is sent in PD1-16 (immunization registry status), with its date in PD1-17: 'A' active, 'I' inactive (reason unspecified), 'L' inactive (lost to follow-up), 'M' inactive (no longer a patient), and 'P' permanently inactive, which MIROW reads as deceased once death is confirmed.		Missing Warning

The guide's patient status sections describe status only in general terms and never name PD1-16 or PD1-17, or say which code means what. The value set (HL70441) has the codes, with 'A' and 'I' required and 'L', 'M', 'P', and 'U' permitted, but a reader of the narrative cannot tell that 'M' is MIROW's "no longer a patient" or that 'P' signals a death.

Recommendation: In Provider-Reported Status, name PD1-16 and PD1-17 and give MIROW's meaning for each HL70441 code (Table 8), including how 'P' relates to the deceased status.

4.5.7.1 Provider-Reported Status, 4.5.7.2 Active and Inactive Status

BR401, BR406 - Designations at the provider organization level

Provider-level status is active, or inactive with one reason: no longer a patient, lost to follow-up, unspecified, or deceased. "Unspecified" is used only when an interface sends inactive without a reason.		Reworded Note

The guide acknowledges the reasons but says they are "often operationally indistinguishable to IIS and should not be over-interpreted". That is consistent with MIROW's own note that many EHRs send no reason.

4.5.7.2 Active and Inactive Status

P312 - Any submission should include patient status

Patient status should be included in any submission from a provider organization to the IIS.		Partly covered Note

The guide treats status as optional in practice: PD1-16 is RE, absence of a status is not evidence either way, and no IIS may make status a condition of acceptance. It does not ask submitters to send it, which MIROW does.

Recommendation: Say that submitters SHOULD send PD1-16 (with PD1-17) when the provider maintains patient status, as MIROW P312 recommends, while keeping the rule that its absence means nothing.

Action: Narrative

4.5.7.1 s1, 4.5.7.2 s1

P308 - Explicit assignment takes precedence

A status explicitly assigned by a provider organization of an acceptable type supersedes its previous status and any status the IIS has inferred, except that a confirmed death is not overridden.		Reworded Note

The guide treats provider-reported active and inactive values as "advisory rather than authoritative", which is weaker than MIROW's supremacy of an explicit assignment at the provider level. Both put deceased first.

Recommendation: CDC to decide whether an explicit PD1-16 value from the provider should replace the provider-level status the IIS holds for that provider (MIROW P308), and say so, or explain why the guide treats it as advisory.

Action: CDC decision

4.5.7.2 s2, 4.5.7.3 s1

BR421, chapter 7 - Deceased p. 56-57

Status becomes inactive (deceased) at both levels only when the death is confirmed. A death sent in PID-29 (date of death) and PID-30 (death indicator) is a confirming message from the provider; a 'P' in PD1-16 without PID-30 'Y' should be investigated, and reminder/recall suppressed meanwhile.		Partly covered Warning

The guide makes deceased status take precedence and says it may be reported by providers or external death reporting, but does not name PID-29 and PID-30, or say how a death is reported in a VXU.

Recommendation: In Deceased Status, state that a provider reports a death in PID-30 ('Y') with the date in PID-29, and point to MIROW BR421 for how an IIS confirms it.

Action: Narrative

4.5.7.3 Deceased Status

P305 - Make status changes available to providers

The IIS should make available to a provider organization the changes it makes to the status of a patient associated with that provider organization.		Partly covered Note

The guide forbids transmitting jurisdictional or IIS-derived statuses in HL7 v2 messages, which is right for the jurisdiction level but leaves no message path for P305 at the provider level (for example, an IIS marking a patient inactive for a provider after a death confirmed by Vital Records). P305 can be met through reports or the user interface, but the guide does not say so.

Recommendation: Add to IIS-Derived Status and Jurisdictional Views that IIS share provider-level status changes they make by other means, such as reports or the user interface (MIROW P305).

Action: Narrative

4.5.7.4 s1

BR411 to BR415, P303, P310 - Jurisdiction-level status

At the jurisdiction level, status is active, inactive (outside jurisdiction), unknown (no address and no vaccination, or no activity for an extended period), or deceased. Non-active status is assigned more cautiously than at the provider level, and a patient living outside the jurisdiction is never active at that level though their provider-level status is still maintained.		Covered

The guide keeps these statuses inside the IIS and out of messages, which is consistent with MIROW giving only the immunization program the right to assign them.

4.5.7.4 IIS-Derived Status and Jurisdictional Views

Chapter 7 - Indirect identification p. 57

An IIS can infer that a provider organization is responsible for a patient from the vaccinations it reports (RXA-3, RXA-9 administered, RXA-11).		Covered

The fields MIROW uses are all required for administered doses; the guide treats the result as IIS-derived status.

4.5.7.4 IIS-Derived Status and Jurisdictional Views, 2.7.5 s1

P311, P309 - All ages; public and private providers alike

Status is maintained for patients of all ages, with the same rules for public and private provider organizations.		Covered

Nothing in the guide limits status by age or provider type.

4.5.7 Patient Active Status in Clinical and IIS Contexts

P313, P314 - Opt-out is not a status

Opting out of the IIS, or of reminder/recall, does not change patient status; it is a separate filter.		Covered

The guide carries consent and protection in PD1-12 and its own sections, separate from status.

4.5.6 Consent and Privacy, 4.5.6.1 Protection Indicator (PD1-12) Usage

Acceptance of vaccinations

(MIROW assumes vaccinations are recorded whatever the patient's status; it does not discuss rejection.)		Guide adds Note

The guide makes explicit that status is never a reason to refuse a vaccination, including historical data for a deceased patient.
