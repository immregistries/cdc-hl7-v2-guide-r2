Vaccination Level Deduplication in Immunization Information Systems

How an IIS selects, evaluates, and resolves possible duplicate vaccination events, including which reported data to keep when two records describe the same dose.

Summary

The guide is built on the same model as this MIROW guide: an IIS receives overlapping and repeated reports, decides which ones describe the same vaccination, and keeps one consolidated event assembled from the best information in each, so submitters need not check before sending and should expect their records to be merged and refined. The guide adds what the 2006 guide did not cover: how a submitter signals a correction (identifying fields, delete followed by add, ORC-3) so that it does not create a duplicate. Two connections need attention. First, the guide makes lot number a non-identifying field that can be corrected with an update, while MIROW treats different lot numbers as strong evidence of different events; an IIS that matches an update by MIROW evaluation rather than by ORC-3 can create the duplicate the guide is trying to prevent. Second, the RXA-9 codes for the source of a historical record carry the confidence information MIROW uses to choose between duplicates, but the narrative never mentions them. The guide also cites this guide for patient deduplication, which it does not cover.

Introduction (scope) p. 1-3

The guide covers deduplication of vaccination events only. Patient-level deduplication (matching patient records) is out of its scope.		Reworded Note

The guide cites this MIROW guide correctly in IIS Matching and Correction Processing, but its reference list ([10]) describes it as guidance on "patient and vaccination record deduplication". Patient deduplication is covered by CDC's Patient-Level De-duplication Best Practices (2013), which the guide does not cite.

Recommendation: Correct the description of reference [10] to vaccination-level deduplication, and cite CDC's Patient-Level De-duplication Best Practices in Record Consolidation and Deduplication.

4.6.4.3 IIS Matching and Correction Processing, 2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication

BR01, BR02 - Selecting candidate duplicates

Vaccination events in the same vaccine family or group within a window of up to 23 days are examined as possible duplicates; each record is compared with all records of the same family, and a combination vaccine is compared with each family it belongs to.		Covered

The guide tells IIS to recognize likely matches that are not exact, such as dates that are close but not identical and vaccine codes that are known equivalents, without prescribing a window.

3.2.4 Identifying vs. Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields, 2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication

BR03 - Identical records

Identical records (same date, provider, and vaccine type) are kept once; providers often resend complete histories, so the same record arrives many times.		Covered

The guide says IIS should expect duplicate reports, and that submitters need not check whether a vaccination is already in the IIS.

4.6.1.2 s4, 2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication

BR09, P04 - Different until proven duplicate

Records are assumed to be different until proven to be duplicates, because a false match loses immunization data while a false non-match only leaves a duplicate. When the evaluation cannot decide, the records go to manual review.		Reworded Note

The guide says high-confidence matches may be merged automatically and ambiguous ones queued for manual review, but not that, when in doubt, keeping both records is the safer error. That bias matters to submitters, who otherwise may expect every resend to collapse into one record.

Recommendation: Add to Record Consolidation and Deduplication that IIS deliberately prefer to keep two records rather than wrongly merge them (MIROW BR09), so occasional duplicates are expected.

Action: Narrative

2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication

BR10 - Different lot numbers

If two records have different lot numbers, they are most likely different vaccination events (allowing for typos and generic entries such as 9999). A matching lot number is not decisive.		Partly covered Warning

The guide classifies lot number as non-identifying: a corrected lot number is sent as an update and SHOULD NOT by itself make the IIS treat the vaccination as new. That works when the IIS finds the earlier report through the submitting source and ORC-3. The guide says ORC-3 matching is not yet consistent, though, and an IIS that falls back to MIROW evaluation will see two records with different lot numbers and, under BR10, keep both. The two documents are not in conflict, but the guide does not say how they fit together.

Recommendation: In IIS Matching and Correction Processing, say that an update is applied to the source record it identifies (by source and ORC-3, or the identifying fields) before deduplication is considered, so that MIROW's lot number rule does not turn a lot number correction into a second event.

Action: Narrative

4.6.4.1 Identifying Field Changes, 3.2.5.3 Modifying Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields, 4.6.4.3 s1

BR11, P11 - Vaccination dates

Records with the same vaccination date are most likely duplicates; when dates differ, their proximity counts (two days apart is more likely a duplicate than 22).		Covered

RXA-3 is an identifying field, and the guide requires it to be accurate enough to support deduplication.

3.2.4 Identifying vs. Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields, 2.8.3 s5

BR15, P13 - Administered or historical

IIS should track whether each record is administered or historical. Two administered records from different providers are most likely different events; an administered and a historical record with close dates are most likely duplicates.		Covered

RXA-9 is required and is an identifying field "because it can affect current IIS vaccination matching behavior", which is this rule.

4.6.4.1 Identifying Field Changes, 4.6.1.6 IIS Processing and Reconciliation, 4.6.1 Administered versus Historical Vaccinations

P10, Table 9 - Confidence in the data

The confidence placed in a record depends on who submitted it and how: a primary submitter reporting administered doses from a clinical system ranks highest, claims data lower, and secondary submitters (historical records) from medium to low, in an order each IIS sets (for example, schools with nurses rank higher).		Partly covered Note

The message carries this information: RXA-9 distinguishes a new administration (00) from historical records by source (from another provider, another registry, a written record, a school record, a birth certificate, a public agency, or the patient's recall, which the value set marks as low reliability). The narrative never mentions these codes or asks submitters to send the most specific one rather than 01 (source unspecified).

Recommendation: In Historical Vaccinations, list the RXA-9 historical source codes, ask submitters to send the most specific source they know, and note that IIS use it to weigh records when deduplicating (MIROW P10).

Action: Narrative

4.6.1.2 Historical Vaccinations, 4.6.1.6 IIS Processing and Reconciliation

P12 - Front-end and back-end processing

Whether a record is incoming or already stored, and whether deduplication runs on receipt or later, makes no difference to whether two records match.		Covered

The guide says duplicates may be resolved during message processing or later by background processes.

2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication

BR20 to BR24 - Selecting the best record

Of two duplicates, select the record with the highest confidence, then the more complete, then the more specific, then the combination vaccine, then the existing record.		Covered

The guide describes the common practice of preferring the administering organization's record, as explanatory guidance; the order of preference is IIS policy.

4.6.1.6 IIS Processing and Reconciliation

BR30 to BR33, P18 - The consolidated record

Merge duplicates into a consolidated vaccination record: keep values they share, prefer known over unknown, prefer the value from the record with higher confidence, and then the more specific value (a full date over month and year).		Covered

The guide adds the message-level consequences: the consolidated event is a different object from each source record, each source keeps its own ORC-3, and submitters should expect returned histories to reflect consolidated data rather than what they sent.

4.6.1.6 IIS Processing and Reconciliation, 4.6.3 Vaccination Record Identity and ORC-3, 2.3.3 Record Consolidation and Deduplication

Use of the consolidated record in query responses

(Not addressed in the 2006 guide, which predates bidirectional query.)		Guide adds Note

The guide returns the consolidated history, without administered/historical distinctions, and the source-assigned ORC-3 when it can.

4.6.1.5 Use of Administered vs. Historical on Output, 4.6.3 s2

Corrections and deletes

(Not addressed in the 2006 guide, which treats every incoming record as a candidate for deduplication.)		Guide adds Note

The guide defines how submitters signal corrections (update for non-identifying fields, delete followed by add for identifying ones, reusing ORC-3), that a delete retracts one source record rather than the consolidated event, and that an IIS must not create a duplicate just because a correction was sent as an add.

3.2.4 Identifying vs. Non-Identifying Vaccination Fields, 4.6.4.2 Submitter Correction Behavior, 4.6.4.4 Handling Delete Requests, 4.6.4.3 s2

Chapter 6 - Provider-related recommendations p. 73-74

Educate providers to reduce duplicate and poorly specified entries: avoid unspecified vaccine codes, report from clinical rather than billing systems, and check charts against the IIS. Pre-qualify each electronic submitter on its first submissions and keep a data quality profile for each.		Covered

The guide's statements on unspecified codes, on not reporting billing dates in RXA-3, on onboarding, and on shared responsibility match these; submitter profiles are IIS practice (see the MIROW data quality guide, BR102).

4.6.2.2.3 s2, 2.8.3 s4, 2.7.2 s3, 2.6.2 Shared Responsibility for Data Quality

Chapter 6 - Invalid doses p. 74-75

A dose repeated because an earlier one was invalid (expired, mishandled, or given incorrectly) is not a duplicate; both doses are kept and the invalid one is flagged.		Covered

The guide keeps every reported vaccination and returns a dose's validity in the evaluation of the RSP, which is how an invalid dose is flagged to a querying system.
