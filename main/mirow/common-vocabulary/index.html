Summary

The guide and the MIROW Common Vocabulary usually use different words for the same things, and the guide does not mention the vocabulary at all. Most differences are naming. The guide's submitting, responsible, administering, and entering organizations are MIROW's submitter, IIS-authorized organization, vaccinator, and recorder. Its funding eligibility and funding source are MIROW's dose-level eligibility and dose-level public/private indicator. Its CVX, NDC, and lot number line up with MIROW's vaccine type, vaccine product type, and vaccine. The guide's choice to avoid "provider" and "site" matches MIROW's reasons for coining provider organization and IIS-AO. A mapping would let readers move between the guide and the MIROW guides, which is where IIS staff find the operational rules. One definition needs work. The guide defines an administered vaccination two ways: in Key Terms by the system that recorded it, and in Administered Vaccinations by the organization that gave it. MIROW's definition (reported by the organization that conducted the vaccination event) matches the second, and so does RXA-9.

The vocabulary as a whole

A shared vocabulary for all MIROW guides, with a definition, comment, aliases, the matching CDC-endorsed data element, and the guides that use each term. AIRA maintains it so that the community uses terms consistently.		Missing Note

Key Terms says its terms "align with common usage in immunization and interoperability communities", and the data model uses the CDC Core Data Elements as its reference catalog. Neither mentions the MIROW Common Vocabulary, although the guide cites MIROW guides whose rules are written in its terms.

Recommendation: In Key Terms, cite the MIROW Common Vocabulary. Add a short table mapping the guide's terms to MIROW's where they differ: submitting organization and submitter; administering organization and vaccinating organization; entering organization and recording organization; organization and provider organization or IIS-AO; funding eligibility and dose-level eligibility; funding source and dose-level public/private indicator; vaccination record and vaccination event.

1.4 Key Terms, 2.3.1 CDC Core Data Elements for Functional Standards

Administered/Historical Indicator

The type of a submission, based on whether it is submitted by the vaccinating organization ("administered") or by a third party ("historical"). Administered means the provider organization recorded or submitted its own vaccination event, attesting that it conducted it. Historical means it submitted a vaccination event conducted by a different provider organization.		Partly covered Warning

The guide has two definitions that don't match. Key Terms defines an administered vaccination as "a dose reported by the system that directly recorded the vaccination event and knows its details". That is about who recorded it. Administered Vaccinations says administered vaccinations "SHALL represent doses given directly by the reporting organization", which is about who gave it. MIROW and RXA-9 code 00 (new immunization record) go with the second. Under the Key Terms wording, a clinic that types another clinic's dose into its own EHR "directly recorded" it.

Recommendation: Change the Key Terms definition of Administered Vaccination to match Administered Vaccinations and MIROW: a dose reported by, or on behalf of, the organization that gave it. Define Historical Vaccination as any dose reported by another party.

Action: Narrative

1.4 Key Terms, 4.6.1.1 s1, 4.6.1 Administered versus Historical Vaccinations

Submitter, Recorder, Vaccinator, IIS-Authorized Organization; notes on provider organization and IIS-AO

An IIS-authorized organization (IIS-AO) has an agreement with an IIS to submit or retrieve information, and always has an IIS-AO ID assigned by the IIS. In a submission an IIS-AO plays one or more roles: recorder (records the information), vaccinator (vaccinates the patient), or submitter (submits to the IIS, or to an intermediary with the IIS as destination). One IIS-AO may submit for another. The IIS needs to know every IIS-AO in the chain of submission. MIROW also notes that the CDC-endorsed definition of responsible organization ("originated and is accountable for the content") should change, because not every IIS-AO is accountable for the content.		Reworded Note

The guide's roles line up with MIROW's: submitter is MSH-4, vaccinator is RXA-11.4, and recorder is ORC-17 (which the guide names "Entering (Recording) Organization"). The IIS assigns every organization identifier. The guide adds the responsible organization (MSH-22) and keeps the "accountable" wording MIROW questioned. When a message passes through an intermediary (an HIE, for example), MSH-4 names the organization on the connection. The message has no place for the other organizations in MIROW's chain of submission, so the IIS has to know them from onboarding.

2.7 Identifying Organizations, 2.7.2 Submitting Organization (MSH-4 Sending Facility), 2.7.3 Responsible Organization (MSH-22), 2.7.5 Administering Organization (RXA-11), 2.7.4 Entering (Recording) Organization (ORC-17), 2.7.6 s1

Provider Organization

An organization that provides vaccination services, is responsible for an entity that does, or manages inventory for one. It may contain units (clinics, sites, departments) with their own IIS-AO IDs.		Covered

The guide uses "organization" for the same scope ("any entity that administers, records, or manages vaccination services") and avoids "provider" and "site" for the same reason MIROW coined its terms. Child organizations are identified in RXA-11 and ORC-17.

1.4 Key Terms, 2.7.6 Parent and Child Organizations

Vaccination Event, Vaccination Encounter

A vaccination event is the administration of one vaccine to a patient. A vaccination encounter is an interaction (such as an office visit) in which zero or more vaccination events occur. Refusals and contraindications are not vaccination events; they belong to the encounter.		Covered

The data model keeps refusals and contraindications out of the vaccination entity. The guide distinguishes the source record each submitter reports from the consolidated vaccination event in the IIS. HL7 sends a refusal in an RXA, but the guide treats it as non-administration, not as a vaccination.

2.3 Data Model, 4.8.1.2 Dose-Level Non-Administration, 4.6.3 Vaccination Record Identity and ORC-3

Immunization Contraindication, Immunization Exemption

An immunization contraindication is a condition in a patient that greatly increases the chance of a serious adverse reaction and makes a vaccination inadvisable. An immunization exemption is a non-medical reason for excluding a patient from a vaccination.		Reworded Note

The guide discourages the unqualified term "contraindication". It treats patient characteristics as conditions and a decision not to give a dose as non-administration. That is a more precise split than MIROW's. A MIROW contraindication is a guide condition, and a MIROW exemption is closest to the guide's refusal.

4.8.3 Terminology Clarification, 4.8.6 Conditions (Contraindications, Precautions, and Indications), 4.8.7.1 Defining a Refusal

Vaccine Type, Vaccine Product Type, Vaccine (note on vaccine-related terms)

A vaccine type is a classification by the disease it protects against (CVX). A vaccine product type adds the manufacturer and presentation (NDC, MVX, trade name). A vaccine is the dose actually given, identified by lot number and expiration date. A report may not carry every level: the CVX may be known but not the lot.		Covered

The guide uses the same three levels (CVX for the clinical concept, NDC for the manufactured product, lot number for the instance) without MIROW's names.

4.6.2.1 Vaccine Code Systems Overview, 4.6.2.3.3 Relationship Between NDC and CVX, 4.6.2.3 NDC - National Drug Code

Dose-Level Eligibility, Dose-Level Public/Private Indicator, Lot-Level Public/Private Indicator

Dose-level eligibility is a patient's eligibility for a funding program, determined for each administered dose. The dose-level public/private indicator is the source of funding for a dose, assigned by the provider organization (the CDC-endorsed "vaccine funding source"). The lot-level indicator is the IIS's designation of an inventory lot.		Reworded Note

The guide's terminology note gives the CDC Core Data Elements names and the LOINC codes, but not MIROW's names, which the decrementing inventory guide uses throughout (see mirow-di-04, mirow-di-05). Covered by the mapping recommended in mirow-cv-01.

4.6.5 Funding Source and Eligibility, 4.6.5.1 Patient Funding Eligibility (Per Dose)

IIS Patient ID, Alternate Patient ID Group, IIS Vaccination Event ID, Alternate Vaccination Event ID

The IIS assigns its own patient and vaccination event IDs. An alternate patient ID group (ID, type, and assigning authority) is assigned by another organization. An alternate vaccination event ID is the submitter's event ID together with the IIS-AO that assigned it. The crosswalk notes that the vaccination event ID is ORC-3 (filler order number) in the HL7 guide.		Covered

PID-3 with identifier type and assigning authority carries the alternate patient ID group. ORC-3 with its assigning authority carries the alternate vaccination event ID, and the IIS's identifier for the consolidated event is returned when the source's is not available.

4.5.1.7 IIS Registry Identifier, 4.5.1.10 Other Patient Identifiers, 4.5.1.2 Assigning Authority and Identifier Namespace, 4.6.3 Vaccination Record Identity and ORC-3

Patient Status at Provider Organization Level, Patient Status at Geographic Jurisdiction Level

Patient status with respect to a provider organization (active, inactive, deceased) or to an immunization program (active, inactive, unknown, deceased). It does not describe whether a patient is up to date on vaccinations.		Covered

The same two levels; see the patient status comparison (mirow-mps-01, mirow-mps-08).

4.5.7.1 Provider-Reported Status, 4.5.7.4 IIS-Derived Status and Jurisdictional Views

Action Code

A value submitted on a vaccination event submission that indicates the desired action on the vaccination event: A (add), D (delete), or U (update).		Covered4.6.4.2 s1, 4.6.4.3 IIS Matching and Correction Processing

Dose Condition

An indicator that a dose administered to a patient is considered substandard.		Partly covered Note

The guide has no way for a submitter to say that a dose it gave was substandard (expired, stored out of range, or given incorrectly). An IIS that learns of it shows it only as an invalid dose in the evaluation of the RSP. MIROW's decrementing guide records such a dose as "administered, non-viable" (S501), and the deduplication guide keeps the repeat dose and flags the invalid one (mirow-vd-15). The usual practice is to report the dose as given and let the IIS or the forecaster judge it, which may be enough.

Recommendation: CDC to decide whether the guide should say how a submitter reports a dose it knows to be substandard (for example, a dose later found to have been stored out of range), or state that this is left to the IIS's evaluation.

Action: CDC decision
