4.5 Patient Information

Quality review 2 warnings · 2 notes

Findings from automated and editorial checks, for the authors. Not part of the guide.

  • Warning MIROW guide Consolidating Demographic Records and Vaccination Event Records (BR1201, BR6001, P10 - Unmerge, and prevent re-merging (twins)): Missing. Unmerging is IIS processing, but the message is where twins are told apart: the multiple birth indicator (PID-24, RE) and birth order (PID-25, conditional). The narrative never mentions them, so submitters have no reason to send them. MIROW also treats them as values from Vital Records, the definitive source (BR601).
    MissingAction: Narrative
    Best practice An IIS should be able to unmerge a consolidated record and should prevent re-merging records it has unmerged, which happens often with twins.
    Recommendation Add a short section on multiple births to Patient Information: send PID-24 and PID-25 whenever known, because they are what keeps an IIS from merging twins, and query with them when known.

    See the MIROW comparison

    qf-mirow-cr-09
  • Warning MIROW guide MIROW Principles and Business Rules Reports (RR2009 - BR401 - Who is included in reminder/recall, including opt-out): Missing. PD1-11 (publicity code) carries the patient's reminder/recall preference. It is RE in the VXU, with twelve required national codes for what is allowed: none, reminder or recall only, by any method or no calls, or to the provider. PD1-18 carries its date. The narrative never mentions PD1-11. Submitters can't tell what the codes mean, that the field is separate from PD1-12 (sharing) and from patient status, or that an empty field means no preference was recorded. MIROW keeps opting out of reminder/recall separate from patient status (MPS2019 P313, P314; see mirow-mps-11).
    MissingAction: Narrative
    Best practice Criteria for including people in reminder/recall include age, the provider association (immunization home), patient status at both levels, vaccines and doses due, risk status, address, program, insurance, exemptions and contraindications, language, and opting out of reminder/recall in whole or in part. The CDC-endorsed "reminder/ recall status" (publicity code) records whether a person wishes to be contacted.
    Recommendation Add a short section on PD1-11 to Patient Information. Say that it records the patient's or guardian's explicit reminder/recall preference, with the date in PD1-18. Say that it is sent only when a preference was expressed, that it is independent of PD1-12 and of patient status, and that IIS use it to filter reminder/recall (MIROW RR2009 BR401).

    See the MIROW comparison

    qf-mirow-pbr-07
  • Note MIROW guide Immunization Information Systems Patient-Level De-duplication Best Practices (3.6.1 - Fields used for matching): Partly covered. The guide covers names, date of birth, address, race, and ethnicity in detail. It doesn't discuss the multiple birth indicator or birth order (PID-24, PID-25), which this report lists as an optional matching field and MIROW's consolidation guide relies on to tell twins apart.
    Partly coveredAction: Narrative
    Best practice Most IIS match on name, date of birth, and gender. Mother's maiden name, address, and vaccination data are common. Optional fields that help include SSN, birth order, race, ethnicity, and local record numbers.
    Recommendation Covered by the recommendation in mirow-cr-09 to add a short section on multiple births.

    See the MIROW comparison

    qf-normative-prose-1497facb

Immunization Information Systems (IIS) receive patient information to fulfill their core responsibility: maintaining a complete, longitudinal immunization record for each individual in their jurisdiction. Because IIS aggregate data from many independent sources over time, they must continuously determine whether an incoming submission refers to an existing person or represents a new individual. Patient information therefore plays a central role in record matching and consolidation, even when individual data elements are incomplete or vary across sources.

Beyond matching, patient information supports additional IIS operational and public health functions. Date of birth enables age-based vaccine evaluation and forecasting; address information supports jurisdictional attribution and identification of geographic pockets of need; phone and email may be used for patient or guardian contact; race and ethnicity support population-level monitoring and identification of coverage gaps; and consent, privacy, and patient status inform how data may be stored, shared, and interpreted. The sections below provide context and guidance for these data elements, clarifying their intended use and expectations beyond basic HL7 v2 structural requirements.
