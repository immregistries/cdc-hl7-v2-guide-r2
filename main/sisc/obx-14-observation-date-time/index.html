Guidance on Populating OBX-14

What the observation date-time (OBX-14) means for the common observation types in VXU and RSP messages, many of which are not physiological.

Published PDF · Copy in this repository

Guide sections it affects

  • 4.4.2.6 OBX-14 Date/Time of the Observation
  • 4.8.6.3 Date Semantics

Summary

The guide's OBX-14 section (4.4.2.6) reproduces this guidance's principles almost word for word, including how OBX-14 relates to RXA-3. It leaves out the table that says which date to use for each common observation (eligibility, funding source, VIS, reaction, comment, evaluation, forecast), and the advice to agree any other meaning with trading partners. It also loosens the basic rule from "when the observation occurred" to "occurred or was recorded", and the section on condition date semantics (4.8.6.3) goes further, defining OBX-14 as the date a condition was recorded. That contradicts this guidance's summary, which says OBX-14 is the time of the observation, not when it is reported.

4.4.2.6 OBX-14 Date/Time of the Observation

Populating OBX-14 (principles) p. 1

should OBX-14 should be when the event (observation) occurred. For observations spanning several days (pregnancy, allergies), use the onset. For serology, use the date/time the blood was drawn.		Reworded Warning

Section 4.4.2.6 says OBX-14 should reflect when the observation "occurred or was recorded", adding "recorded". Section 4.8.6.3 says OBX-14 "represents the date the observation was authored or recorded" and "does not indicate when the underlying condition began", except for ongoing conditions. This guidance's summary is explicit that OBX-14 reflects the time of the observation "and not when the message is created".

Recommendation: Drop "or recorded" in 4.4.2.6, and align 4.8.6.3 with it: OBX-14 is when the observation occurred (the onset for ongoing conditions). If CDC prefers the recording date for conditions, say so in 4.4.2.6 as an exception.

4.4.2.6 OBX-14 Date/Time of the Observation

Populating OBX-14 (principles) p. 1-2

RXA-3 and OBX-14 are not always the same: for patient-level observations RXA-3 is when the report is made; for administration-level observations (including evaluation in an RSP) it is the administration date; for forecast observations it is when the evaluation and forecast were done. An RSP echoes the date/time the submitter sent in the VXU.		Covered

Reproduced as a list in 4.4.2.6.

4.4.2.6 OBX-14 Date/Time of the Observation, 4.4.1 Overview of LOINC and OBX Usage in Immunization Messaging

Guidance for specific observation types (table) p. 2

OBX-14 by observation: serological immunity, the date the specimen was collected; history of disease, the disease onset; eligibility, the date it was calculated; funding source, the date it was determined; VIS, the date presented; reaction, the date of the reaction; comment, the date entered or updated; evaluation (RSP), the date performed; forecast (RSP), the date generated.		Partly covered Warning

The guide gives the rule for serology and multi-day conditions, but not the table. Without it, senders must guess the date for eligibility, funding source, VIS, comments, evaluations, and forecasts.

Recommendation: Add the table to 4.4.2.6 (it is short), or put the date in each observation's row in Figure 20.
