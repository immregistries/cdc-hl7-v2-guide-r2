SISC guidance · October 2015

Guidance for HL7 Acknowledgement Messages to Support Interoperability

Based on a 2015 review of ACKs from 21 IIS, clarifies how to populate MSA-1, ERR-2, ERR-3, ERR-4, ERR-5, and ERR-8 so that an ACK tells the sender plainly whether the message was accepted and what, if anything, must be fixed.

Published PDF · Copy in this repository

The ACK TAB (June 2026) updates and replaces much of this guidance; compare only what the TAB did not carry over. A Word copy marked v1.1 (January 2018) also exists; the published PDF (last updated October 2015) is the one compared.

Summary

Everything in this guidance that the ACK TAB kept is in the guide, often in stronger form: the meaning of each ERR-4 severity and what the sender must do about it, each IIS deciding the severity of its own rules, and correcting before resubmitting (including that the fault may be on the IIS side). The one deliberate reversal is MSA-1. In 2015, 'AE' was returned for any Warning or Error, so a rejected message also got 'AE', and 'AR' was kept for four HL7 conditions. Under the guide (following the ACK TAB), 'AE' means accepted with warnings, 'AR' means rejected, and one of the four conditions is no longer a reason for 'AR'. Many IIS and EHRs implemented the 2015 rules, so the guide's change history should say this plainly.

Scope of Guidance p. 1

An ACK has exactly one MSA segment, followed by ERR segments, which may repeat (usage RE). MSA-1 and ERR-4 are the two fields that tell the sender whether processing succeeded.		Covered3.3.3 Issue-Level Reporting Using ERR Segments, 3.3.4 MSA-1: Derivation of Message-Level Disposition

Vocabulary p. 1-2

"Error" can mean something wrong in general, the ERR segment, the ERR-4 severity 'E', or the MSA-1 code 'AE'; the guidance tries to say which meaning it intends.		Covered

The guide avoids the ambiguity by calling what an ERR segment reports an "issue" and keeping "Error" for the ERR-4 severity.

ERR-4 (Severity) p. 2

must Each IIS decides how severe a given condition is (one IIS may treat an invalid route as an Error, another as a Warning), but what Error, Warning, and Information mean, and what the sender is expected to do, must be the same across IIS. ERR-4 (Severity), Table 2 p. 3

Information: the sender need not correct or resubmit. Warning: the sender must correct but need not resubmit. Error: the sender must correct and resubmit.		Covered

The guide keeps the same ladder and adds "convey" (showing the issue to users) as a step below correcting.

3.3.3.2 ERR-4: Severity Code, 3.3.3.3 Required Follow-Up Actions

ERR-4 (Severity) p. 2-3

A Warning in one ERR segment does not mean the message was accepted: another ERR segment may hold an Error, so the sender must check them all.		Covered

The guide solves the same problem the other way round: MSA-1 now reliably says whether the message was accepted, and ERR-4 says what to do about each issue.

3.3.4 MSA-1: Derivation of Message-Level Disposition, 3.3.4.1 MSA-1 Guidance

MSA-1 (Acknowledgement Code), Tables 4 and 5 p. 3-5

MSA-1 is 'AA' when there are no ERR segments or only Information; 'AE' when at least one ERR segment is a Warning or an Error (so a rejected message also gets 'AE'); and 'AR' only when an Error is due to an unsupported message type, event code, or processing ID, or the message cannot be processed for reasons unrelated to format or content.		Conflict Note

Changed on purpose, following the ACK TAB. In the guide, 'AE' SHALL NOT be returned when there is an Error, 'AR' is returned whenever core intent is not fulfilled (any Error), and "unable to process for reasons unrelated to format or content" is no longer a reason for 'AR'. The guide's MSA-1 guidance explains the change but does not name this document. IIS and senders that built to the 2015 rules will send or expect 'AE' for rejected messages.

Recommendation: In the change history, record that the MSA-1 rules replace those in the 2015 ACK guidance (and Release 1.5): 'AE' no longer covers rejections. Consider a short migration note for systems that treat 'AE' as a rejection.

Action: Narrative

3.3.4 MSA-1: Derivation of Message-Level Disposition, 3.3.4 s2, 3.3.4.1 MSA-1 Guidance

Correction and Resubmission p. 4

An ACK cannot say what data was consumed. The sender investigates each Error, corrects the issue in its own system, and resubmits the corrected data. Correcting may need a conversation with the IIS, since the problem may be on the IIS side (for example, a new CVX code the IIS does not yet accept).		Covered

The guide says resubmission follows correcting the issue "regardless of where it originated", and that a message needing resubmission means "at least one system, on either or both sides, must investigate". What exactly to resend is a gap noted in the ACK TAB comparison.
