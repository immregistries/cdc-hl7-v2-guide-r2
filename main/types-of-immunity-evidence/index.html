4.8.8.1 Types of Immunity Evidence

Quality review 1 warning · 2 notes

Findings from automated and editorial checks, for the authors and reviewers. Not part of the guide.

  • Warning Statement s4 Statement combines MAY, SHALL. Each statement should carry one requirement so it can be tested and referenced on its own.qf-multi-verb-statement-80640372
  • Note Statement s4 Its subject is "It", not a system or party, so it is unclear who must comply. Naming the responsible party (for example, "Submitting systems SHALL …" or "IIS SHALL …") makes the requirement testable.qf-no-actor-3964340d
  • Note 4 requirements were written as paragraphs in the draft and are shown here as conformance statements (wording unchanged): s1, s2, s3, s4.qf-statement-from-paragraph-a4857c6d

Two forms of immunity information may be reported: a clinician-validated history of disease, and serological evidence demonstrating immunity to a specific pathogen. Both represent clinical determinations but differ in strength. Serology generally provides stronger evidence of immunity, while history of disease reflects a documented diagnosis accepted by the clinician or the submitting system. IIS vary in whether they store either or both.

While implementation varies, documented immunity evidence is clinically important and can materially affect individual forecasting and public-health decision-making; the guide therefore encourages consistent reporting and clear documentation of how such evidence is used.

Conformance statements

  1. MAYSubmitting systems MAY send history of disease or serological immunity evidence.s1
  2. MAYIIS MAY store history of disease or serological immunity evidence.s2
  3. SHALL NOTIIS SHALL NOT reject a message solely because immunity information is included.s3
  4. MAYIf an IIS cannot store immunity information, it MAY issue an informational message but SHALL continue normal processing.⚑s4
