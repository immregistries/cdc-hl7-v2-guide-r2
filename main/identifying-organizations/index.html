2.7 Identifying Organizations

Quality review 1 note

Findings from automated and editorial checks, for the authors. Not part of the guide.

  • Note MIROW guide MIROW Common Vocabulary (Submitter, Recorder, Vaccinator, IIS-Authorized Organization; notes on provider organization and IIS-AO): Reworded. The guide's roles line up with MIROW's: submitter is MSH-4, vaccinator is RXA-11.4, and recorder is ORC-17 (which the guide names "Entering (Recording) Organization"). The IIS assigns every organization identifier. The guide adds the responsible organization (MSH-22) and keeps the "accountable" wording MIROW questioned. When a message passes through an intermediary (an HIE, for example), MSH-4 names the organization on the connection. The message has no place for the other organizations in MIROW's chain of submission, so the IIS has to know them from onboarding.
    Reworded
    Best practice An IIS-authorized organization (IIS-AO) has an agreement with an IIS to submit or retrieve information, and always has an IIS-AO ID assigned by the IIS. In a submission an IIS-AO plays one or more roles: recorder (records the information), vaccinator (vaccinates the patient), or submitter (submits to the IIS, or to an intermediary with the IIS as destination). One IIS-AO may submit for another. The IIS needs to know every IIS-AO in the chain of submission. MIROW also notes that the CDC-endorsed definition of responsible organization ("originated and is accountable for the content") should change, because not every IIS-AO is accountable for the content.

    qf-mirow-cv-03

Accurate identification of organizations involved in immunization reporting is foundational to IIS operations. IIS rely on these identifiers to attribute data correctly, manage accountability, support program operations, and troubleshoot data quality issues. Incorrect or ambiguous organization identification can undermine trust in the data and create downstream operational risk.

Submitting systems and healthcare organizations are structured in many different ways. A single clinic may operate independently, or it may function as one unit within a larger parent organization such as a health system, pharmacy chain, or public health program. Technically, multiple organizations may share a single software instance or gateway, while legally they remain distinct entities. These organizational and technical structures do not always align with how IIS assign identifiers, creating the need for clear conventions and shared understanding.

This section defines how healthcare organizations are identified in immunization messages, how roles are distinguished, and how parent-child organizational relationships are represented. The goal is not to model every local hierarchy, but to ensure that each message clearly communicates who submitted the data, who is responsible for it, and when applicable, who administered the vaccination.
