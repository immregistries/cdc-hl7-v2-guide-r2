IIS responses should be designed to minimize disclosure risk while still allowing querying systems and users to confidently interpret the result of a query. The information returned by the IIS serves two distinct purposes: to deliver immunization history and clinical decision support results, and to provide sufficient patient context for a user to confirm that the correct record has been retrieved.

Patient demographic information returned in a response is intended for confirmation, not for synchronization or replacement of local patient records. An IIS may be confident in the strength of a match, but the querying system user must be able to review key identifying attributes to verify that the record corresponds to the intended patient. As a result, the patient information returned by the IIS may differ from what was submitted in the query and should not be assumed to be more current or authoritative.

While IIS work to maintain accurate patient identity and contact information, they are not designed to function as comprehensive or current patient identity repositories. Address, phone number, and similar fields may be incomplete, outdated, duplicated, or inconsistent across submissions. Querying systems should not treat IIS responses as a source for updating local patient demographic data; the purpose of the query is to retrieve immunization history, evaluation, and recommendations, not to reconcile patient contact information.

IIS should carefully consider which patient attributes are returned and limit disclosure to information that materially supports record confirmation. Some data elements may contribute to internal matching processes while providing little additional value to the querying user, and it is neither necessary nor desirable to return all data used for matching as part of a response. For example, an IIS may use mother's maiden name internally as a high-confidence matching element but intentionally withhold that value from RSP responses, returning only less sensitive confirmation attributes (for example, partial address or birth date) to support user verification while reducing disclosure risk. The use of a field to support internal matching does not imply that the same field should be disclosed externally; IIS should balance confirmation needs against disclosure risk and design response content to minimize unnecessary exposure of patient information.
