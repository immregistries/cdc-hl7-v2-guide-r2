2.6 Data Quality Processes

High-quality immunization information depends on more than technical message validation. While HL7 conformance checks ensure that messages meet the structural requirements of the interface specification, data quality is a broader, end-to-end process that spans clinical workflows, correct system configuration, effective onboarding of submitting systems, and ongoing monitoring long after an HL7 interface is certified. This guide treats data quality as a shared, continuous responsibility between submitting systems, vendors, and IIS programs, focused on producing data that is accurate, complete, timely, and usable for public health and clinical decision-making.

IIS programs improve data quality by focusing on three foundational dimensions, accuracy, completeness, and timeliness, and by ensuring that issues detectable at message receipt are communicated early through acknowledgement feedback, while also feeding into longer-term data-quality monitoring and improvement activities.

Message rejection alone is not an effective data-quality strategy. IIS programs typically rely on a combination of message feedback, reporting mechanisms, and coordinated follow-up with submitters, vendors, and IIS staff to improve the overall accuracy, completeness, and timeliness of immunization data over time. Feedback provided at message receipt works best when it aligns with, and feeds into, these ongoing monitoring processes, rather than functioning as a standalone check on data quality.
