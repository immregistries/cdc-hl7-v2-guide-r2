Program requirements are often expressed in plain language: "Providers must collect X," "Programs must report Y," etc. HL7 v2 uses conformance verbs, R, RE, O, CE, that have precise, testable meanings. When implementers copy policy language directly into HL7 tables, they typically convert programmatic requirements into R fields. This is wrong. It breaks interoperability, produces filler data, complicates onboarding, and often fails to meet the original policy intent.

The guiding principle is simple: do not make a field Required (R) solely because someone believes it will produce better data. It won't.

Informative Example: A program may require that providers collect patient race for reporting, but translating that policy into an HL7 field marked Required (R) is problematic; it forces senders to populate the element even when the information is unknown, which can lead to filler values, message rejection, or inaccurate data. Instead, the field should be RE (Required, but may be Empty if unknown) so that systems only send race when the data are actually available. To improve capture rates, jurisdictions should pursue operational measures, such as training and data-quality monitoring, rather than converting policy into an unconditional R field.
