3.2.5.7 Official Registry Name Recorded for a Newborn

Accurate identification of newborns is essential for reliable patient matching and consolidation within an IIS. In many clinical systems, newborns are initially assigned temporary or placeholder names as an operational convenience, and immunizations administered during this period may be reported before an official registry name, or previously legal name, is recorded. The official registry is the name as it appears on official documents, such as a birth certificate, driver's license, or passport, and the recorded name should match as closely as possible (see Section 4.5.2 Patient and Other Names). If the official registry is never transmitted, subsequent immunization data may not be reliably linked to the existing patient record, resulting in fragmented histories and inflated counts of partially vaccinated newborns.

When the official registry name is recorded for a newborn for the first time, it represents a significant improvement in patient identity quality and must be communicated to the IIS. Submitting systems SHALL trigger a VXU containing the patient's complete immunization history at that time, even if some or all of those immunizations were previously reported. This ensures that the IIS can correctly associate vaccinations with the newly identified patient record, whether by updating an existing record or establishing a new one as needed.

This guidance is not intended to dictate naming practices or the timing of name assignment, but to ensure that once an official registry name is available, the IIS receives sufficient information to consolidate the patient record and preserve an accurate immunization history.
