4.5.2.8 Special Characters

Names are increasingly recorded with diacritics and extended Latin characters. IIS must be prepared to handle these characters correctly when receiving HL7 v2 messages. The intent is to preserve accurate representation of names while maintaining interoperability with systems that cannot store these characters natively.

This guidance applies only to the extended Latin alphabet. Support for non-Latin scripts (such as Arabic, Cyrillic, or Chinese) is outside the scope of this Implementation Guide.

This approach ensures consistent and predictable treatment of special characters across all IIS implementations while allowing flexibility for systems with broader Unicode support.

Conformance statements

  1. SHALLSystems SHALL support the following 18 commonly used Latin-based characters (in both upper and lowercase): á, â, à, é, ê, ë, è, í, î, ï, ó, ô, ú, û, ü, ù, ç, ñ.s1
  2. SHALLSystems SHALL either preserve these characters as submitted or transliterate them using the conversion table provided in this guide.s2
  3. MAYSystems MAY support additional special characters beyond this list.s3
  4. SHALL NOTIIS SHALL NOT reject a message solely because one of these characters appears in a patient or person name.s4
  5. SHALLIIS SHALL apply transliteration rules consistently when storage systems cannot represent these characters.s5
