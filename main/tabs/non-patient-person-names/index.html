Technical Advisory Bulletin · July 2025

Messaging Non-patient Person Names in HL7

Extends the patient name work to mother's maiden name (PID-6, QPD-5), responsible person (NK1-2), and the provider names in ORC-10, ORC-12, and RXA-10.

Published PDF · Copy in this repository

Guide sections it affects

  • 4.5.2.11 Non-Patient Person Names
  • 4.5.2.8 Special Characters

Summary

Section 4.5.2.11 (Non-Patient Person Names) carries this short TAB over almost word for word: spaces and hyphens, special characters, case, 64 characters, and the NK1-2 name type rules, with the 'L'-first requirement retired. IGAMT agrees (NK1-2.7 is required). One error: the guide lists "ORC-1 Entered By"; Entered By is ORC-10. The TAB's table of which fields apply to VXU, QBP, and RSP is not carried over.

Findings: 1 warning
Results: Conflict 1 · Covered 6

Reviewed 2026-11-16 against the narrative draft of 4 Nov 2026 and IGAMT export of 6 Nov 2026. Recorded in quality/tabs/non-patient-person-names.yml.

Requirement by requirement

The TAB saysResultIn the guide

Scope (Table 1) p. 2-3

The fields covered: PID-6 Mother's Maiden Name (VXU, QBP as QPD-5, RSP), NK1-2 Responsible Person Name, ORC-10 Entered By, ORC-12 Ordering Provider, and RXA-10 Administering Provider (VXU and RSP).		Conflict Warning

The guide's list has "ORC-1 Entered By". ORC-1 is the Order Control code; Entered By is ORC-10. The other four fields are right. The guide does not say which message types each field applies to.

Recommendation: Change "ORC-1" to "ORC-10", and add the TAB's Table 1 (field by VXU, QBP, RSP).

4.5.2.11 Non-Patient Person Names

Clarifications and Conformance Expectations p. 3

SHALL For these five fields, systems SHALL preserve spaces and hyphens.		Covered4.5.2.11 s1

Clarifications and Conformance Expectations (Table 2) p. 3-4

SHALL Systems SHALL support commonly used special characters by storing them as is or, if necessary, downgrading them following the transliterations in Table 2 (the same 18 characters as the PID-5 TAB).		Covered

The guide says "commonly used diacritics or transliterations" and relies on 4.5.2.8 (Special Characters), which covers "a patient or person name", for the list and the table.

4.5.2.11 s2, 4.5.2.8 s1, 4.5.2.8 s2, 4.5.2.8 s4

Clarifications and Conformance Expectations p. 3

SHOULD Systems SHOULD support preservation of case, and names with up to 64 characters in the first, middle, and last name components.		Covered4.5.2.11 s3

Additional specifications for NK1-2 p. 4

SHOULD, SHALL Submitting systems SHOULD send the most reliable and commonly used responsible person name, preferably the official registry name ('L'), and SHALL populate NK1-2.7 to reflect the type of name in each NK1-2 repetition.		Covered

The guide says "SHOULD use the official registry ('L') name when known", without "the most reliable and commonly used name". IGAMT makes NK1-2.7 required (XPN_IZ01), which matches the SHALL.

4.5.2.11 s4

Implications p. 4

The PID-5 specifications extend to these fields where applicable. For NK1-2, 'L' no longer has to be in the first repetition (backward compatibility to HL7 v2.2 is dropped).		Covered4.5.2.11 Non-Patient Person Names

Out of Scope p. 3

Which name type codes beyond 'L' are valid in NK1-2, and deduplication rules, are out of scope.		Covered

The guide is likewise silent on NK1-2 name types. In IGAMT, NK1-2 uses XPN_IZ01, the official registry name flavor (given and family name required) for every repetition, whatever its name type; the TAB does not address this.
