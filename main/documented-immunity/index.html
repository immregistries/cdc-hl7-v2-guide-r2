4.8.8 Documented Immunity

Immunity information represents clinical evidence that a patient has acquired protection against a vaccine-preventable disease through means other than a documented vaccination series. This includes both documented history of disease and serological findings indicating immunity. These observations may help an IIS or forecasting system understand whether a vaccination recommendation remains clinically appropriate.

Immunity information is recognized in the CDC Core Data Elements (for example, Evidence of Immunity and Date of Immunity Evidence). However, collection, storage, and operational use of documented immunity vary across jurisdictions and systems. Some IIS accept and store these observations; others do not store or operationalize them. Their presence in a message does not imply that the IIS will change evaluation or forecasting logic. Decisions about whether immunity affects recommendations depend entirely on local policy and the clinical decision support system in use.

For the purposes of this guide, immunity refers only to explicit clinical evidence of disease history or laboratory evidence of immunity. It does not include presumed immunity derived from a completed vaccination series; that determination is made dynamically by forecasting logic rather than stored as an observation.
