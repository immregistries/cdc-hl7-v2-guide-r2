4.6.2.4.3 NDC Requirements for Administered Vaccinations

Quality review 1 warning · 3 notes

Findings from automated and editorial checks, for the authors. Not part of the guide.

  • Warning Statement s9 MIROW guide Immunization Information System Inventory Management Operations (BR704 (remarks), chapter 7 - Multi-component vaccines): Partly covered. The guide tells submitters to report the NDC of the lyophilized powder for reconstituted vaccines, and any NDC for other multi-component products. It says nothing about which lot number goes in RXA-15. RXA-15 repeats (1..*) in the administered RXA, so a submitter could send every component's lot, the outer package's, or one chosen at random. MIROW's answer is one lot number and one NDC from the outside of the package. The guide's NDC rule picks the powder, which serves product identification but not inventory. The same RXA-15 gap was found in the SISC and consolidation comparisons (sisc-lot-01, mirow-cr-08).
    Partly coveredAction: CDC decision
    Best practice Each component of a multi-component vaccine (one with a diluent or components that are combined) has its own lot number. There is always one lot number and one NDC, on the outside of the package, that characterize the vaccine as a whole, and those are used for inventory. For a product such as Pentacel, with separate NDCs for powder and liquid, the only NDC that can be used for ordering, inventory, and returns is the one on the CDC contract.
    Recommendation CDC to decide which lot number a submitter sends in RXA-15 for a vaccine with several components: the one on the outer package that the IIS inventory knows (MIROW BR704), or the active component's, to match the NDC rule. Say so with the RXA-15 cardinality fix.

  • Note Statement s9 SISC guidance Guidance on Unit of Sale/Unit of Use Lot Numbers (Notes: Matching, Reconstituted Vaccines, and Regular Expression): Partly covered. The guide requires the NDC of the lyophilized powder rather than the diluent, but does not say the same for the lot number. A submitter could report the diluent's lot with the powder's NDC.
    Partly coveredAction: Narrative
    Guidance For reconstituted vaccines, the unit-of-sale lot and the lot on the lyophilized vaccine powder are usually the same; the liquid diluent typically has a different lot number.
    Recommendation Extend the reconstitution statement to the lot number: report the lot of the lyophilized powder (the active product), not the diluent.

Conformance statements

  1. SHALLSubmitting systems SHALL support Unit-of-Use NDCs for administered vaccinations.s1
  2. SHOULDSubmitting systems SHOULD support Unit-of-Sale NDCs for administered vaccinations.s2
  3. SHALLIIS SHALL accept both Unit-of-Use and Unit-of-Sale NDCs when reported.s3
  4. SHALLSubmitting systems SHALL support NDC11 formatted with dashes using the 5-4-2 format (xxxxx-xxxx-xx).s4
  5. SHOULD NOTSubmitting systems SHOULD NOT use NDC10 without dashes.s5
  6. MAYSubmitting systems MAY support NDC11 without dashes.s6
  7. MAYSubmitting systems MAY support NDC10 with dashes using the 4-4-2 format.s7
  8. SHALLIIS SHALL be capable of accepting and interpreting commonly used NDC formats.s8
  9. SHALLFor vaccines requiring reconstitution, submitting systems SHALL report the NDC for the lyophilized powder (the active product) rather than the diluent.⚑s9
  10. SHALLFor vaccines with multiple active components, submitting systems SHALL report any NDC associated with the vaccine (Unit-of-Use or Unit-of-Sale), consistent with clinical workflow.s10
  11. SHALLIIS SHALL accept administered vaccination reports for multi-component products without requiring a specific NDC selection.s11
