4.6.1.1 Administered Vaccinations

Quality review 1 warning · 4 notes

Findings from automated and editorial checks, for the authors. Not part of the guide.

  • Warning Statement s1 TAB Submission of Self-Administered FluMist Vaccine to IIS (Background; RXA-9 and RXA-10): Missing. The guide does not mention self- or caregiver-administered vaccines. Its rule that "Administered vaccinations SHALL represent doses given directly by the reporting organization" does not fit a dose the pharmacy dispensed and the patient gave, and a reader could conclude these doses must be reported as historical. IGAMT accepts the TAB's values (RXA-10 is RE with XCN_IZ01; 'MR' is R in HL70203_IZ01).
    MissingAction: CDC decision
    TAB Self- or caregiver-administered doses are reported with RXA-9 '00' (administered, new record). RXA-10 is the patient if self-administered (echoing PID-3) or the caregiver, with identifier type 'MR'. RXA-3 is the date reported by the patient or caregiver.
    Recommendation Decide how self- and caregiver-administered doses are reported in general, then add it to 4.6.1: for example, "A dose dispensed by the reporting organization and administered by the patient or a caregiver is reported as administered (RXA-9 '00'), with the patient or caregiver in RXA-10." Note that CVX 333 already identifies the self- or caregiver-administered product.

    See the TAB comparison

  • Note Statement s2 MIROW guide Data Quality Assurance in Immunization Information Systems (BR122 - Vaccine has vaccine product type): Reworded. The guide asks for complete product information, including NDC, on administered records when available, and requires submitters to support NDCs, but an IIS must accept an administered dose with only a CVX. That is the message-level form of a completeness rule, not a conflict.
    Reworded
    Best practice A vaccination event record should include the vaccine product type (NDC).

    See the MIROW comparison

    qf-mirow-dqa-19
  • Note Statement s2 MIROW guide Immunization Information System Inventory Management Operations (P702, BR704 - A lot number for every administered dose): Reworded. RXA-15 is required in the administered RXA and optional in the historical RXA, which matches MIROW. But the guide does not let an IIS reject a vaccination only because the lot number is missing, unless the IIS has declared it Critical Data. The later MIROW guides take the same position: the 2016 decrementing guide records the vaccination and skips decrementing (S704), and the 2022 data quality guide makes the lot number recommended (BR131). So MIROW's 2012 rejection rule has been superseded.
    Reworded
    Best practice Every administered dose must be documented and reported with its lot number, and a report without one is rejected. Historical doses do not need one. Providers should adopt barcoding and drop-down lists so the lot number is recorded correctly.

    See the MIROW comparison

  • Note Statement s1 Its subject is "Administered vaccinations", not a system or party, so it is unclear who must comply. Naming the responsible party (for example, "Submitting systems SHALL …" or "IIS SHALL …") makes the requirement testable.qf-no-actor-a1b1d926

An administered vaccination is one given directly by the reporting organization. These records are first-hand clinical events and are typically captured as part of formal care workflows.

Administered records are expected to contain more complete and specific data and may drive additional IIS processing such as inventory decrementing or enhanced validation.

Conformance statements

  1. SHALLAdministered vaccinations SHALL represent doses given directly by the reporting organization.⚑s1
  2. SHOULDAdministered records SHOULD include more complete product information when available (e.g., specific CVX, manufacturer, lot number, NDC).⚑s2
  3. MAYIIS MAY apply additional data quality or inventory rules to administered vaccinations.s3
